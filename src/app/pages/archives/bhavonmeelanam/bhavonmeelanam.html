Bhavonmeelanam - when dance meets emotion.

Sharayu Gavi’s abhinaya shows the true essence of emotional expression through dance!

What is the main purpose of dance? It’s about evoking emotions as well as displaying them, but what are emotions without meaning? It's the artform of dance that brings out these emotions, some meaning, some purpose, and it evokes something, a feeling of light in audiences’ mind, but also the dancer’s.

Based on these beautiful thoughts, was the Bharatanatyam recital by Sharayu Gavi at the Mysore Association in Matunga. Actually, it’s a very interesting story of how I met Sharayu.I had gone to Priyadarshini Govind’s recital at the NMACC last month, and that is where she was seated next to me. She recognised me because of my reels on Instagram and immediately said hello, and then we started talking, and then she told me that she was going to have her recital on 9 May in the coming month, and I was very happy to go and I did.

Sharayu is a double graduate in commerce and law, and currently she serves as a legal manager at JioHotstar, but her journey in Bharatanatyam began in 2015, under the guidance of her Guru Nisha Gilbert. For Sharayu dance is not just simply an art form, but it is more than that. It’s a sort of meditation and expression, and a personal sanctuary, which brings her constant joy and a sense of healing. She has performed at several occasions with her teachers troop in Maharashtra and South of India. In 2024, she stepped into the role of a faculty member at the Nupur School of Bharatanatyam. She is now a teacher. For her Bharatanatyam gives a way to live thousands of emotions and express and experience stories far beyond her own reality.

Sharayu has learnt under her Guru Smt. Nisha Gilbert, who is a distinguished Bharatanatyam dancer, choreographer, actor and as well as a columnist. She graduated with a gold medal in Bharatanatyam from the Bharatha College in Mumbai, which is affiliated to Nagpur University. She has won multiple accolades, such as the Nritya Ankur Award, Best Vanita Award, Prawasi Nrityashree, and many more. Her choreography brings in an advanced sense of emotional expression, which was very much vibrantly visible in Sharayu’s performance.

The performance started with Guru Nisha Gilbert, Sharayu‘s parents as well as the Chief Guest Bhushan Koregaonkar lighting the auspicious lamp. Sharayu was joined with a beautiful ensemble of musicians with Dr. Omanakuttan Nair on the vocals, Shri R.Shaktidharan on the Mrudangam, Shri. E.P. Parameshwaran on the violin and Shri. Raghavendra Baliga on the flute. The nattuvangam was done by Srimathi Nisha Gilbert herself.

The performance began with a traditional Pushpanjali, which is an invocatory piece. The Pushpanjali was set in Gambhira Nattai Ragam and Adi Thaalam with a shloka following on Lord Ganesha and Goddess Saraswati. This was a beautiful example of pure dance as well as the Abhinaya side of dance. The margam continued with a traditional piece again, which was the Jathiswaram, which is a pure nritta composition, and this one was set in the Rasikapriya Ragam and Adi Thaalam. The anchor of the show mentioned that Shri Lalgudi Jayaraman compositions were taken for this margam, with multiple compositions of his making appearances in this traditional performance. Through the Jathiswaram, Sharayu was able to show her traditional and technical Bharatanatyam skills, which are very important for creating the base of the dance itself.

The next composition was a padam by Tulsidas titled Thumaka Chalata Ramachandra. This is a padam that celebrates motherhood of Kaushalya and the love of a mother for her child who learnt to walk. It was very interesting to see a padam performed before a varnam, but I think it really helped soothe the margam after the jathiswaram. Sharayu's expressions were beautiful and very realistic in nature. It really felt as if she was Kaushalya, and there was a child Rama in front of her, taking care of him. Her abhinaya and the realistic nature of abhinaya truly made this particular piece magical.

After the beautiful padam was a very surprising element that I have never seen in recitals where the musicians, that is the violinist, the flautist and the Mrudangam artist had composed a small Pallavi on the theme of Bhaavonmeelanam. And it is very commendable and a must for me to note and tell everyone that this Pallavi and the entire composition was composed the day before the performance. It was a beautiful musical performance by the musicians.

A Varnam is the central piece in any Bharatanatyam performance. It is a piece that shows the two sides of dance that is Nritta and Natya. This varnam again is a composition by Lalgudi Jayaraman, and is a personal favourite of mine, set to Raag Charukesi, Innum Manam Me.

In this Varnam the nayika (heroine) is a Virahoth Khanditha Nayika meaning that she is someone who is longing for her beloved friend Krishna. Through this piece, she calls out her friend for Krishna to come and meet her. Whenever I think of this varnam, the name that comes to my mind is Harinie Jeevitha because when she performs she just doesn’t dance, she makes you feel the dance and feel those emotions. which is very similar to what shadow you did,Sharayu very expressively showed the state of the nayika, especially with the help of her sanchari, which is very interesting to note. In the sanchari, she showed the vastraharan of Draupadi. Sharayu takes this Sanchari and asks Krishna that, “If you can come in a matter of seconds to help your sister, then why can’t you come to me if I am calling you for so many years?”

Her dance was very technical and metaphorical in a way that there were many metaphors used by her and her guru to compare situations, and that feeling of extreme longing. It was a beautiful performance, truly showing and emoting the emotions perfectly..

As mentioned earlier, I met Sharayu at Priyadarshini Govind ma’am’s recital at the NMACC and one more connection that I could make in Sharayu’s performance from two things rather, with her performance of Thumaka Chalata Ramachandra, which was performed by Priyadarshini Govind and another one, the next performance title, Ravana, which was again performed by Priyadarshini Govind. It was very interesting for me to see these connections. In this particular performance on Ravaan, Sharayu showcased the Navarasas through each of the heads of Ravana. An important note is that the Keerthanam was written by Parameshwaran Sir. Through her dance, she showed each of the heads displaying one of the nine emotions mentioned in the Natya Shastra. This is where it truly showed Sharayu's innate detail on Abhinaya. The Keerthanam was followed by the Shiv Tandava Stotram written by Ravana in order to please his Lord, Lord Shiva. The Tandava Stotram gained a lot of momentum and immediately drew in the rasikas.

There was a small tea break after this performance. Following the tea break was an interesting performance on a folklore from Northern Kerala titled Muchoilottu Bhagavathi and Poomathe, which brought in the lyrical side to the evening with Malayalam lyrics which praise the strength, wisdom and beauty of women against patriarchy. The composition talked about two women who resisted patriarchy and in turn became beacons of divinity and strength for women.

Another folk composition is a Kavadichandu, a genre of music born out of devotees who used to sing these whilst trekking the hills of Palani and Tiruchendur. This particular Kavadichandu was titled Valli Kanavan Perai, celebrating Muruga. This was a very peppy and fast paced performance which brought out the traditional side of Tamil folk music.

I was very curious to know what the padam would be post the margam and I was very pleased to know that it was an Ashtapadi from Jayadeva’s array of writings, titled. ‘Sakhe Keshi Mathanam Udaram’. Sharayu portrayed the memories of Radha of her union with Krishna, as she tells her Sakhi her secret under the moonlit atmosphere of Vrindavan. Jayadeva’s Ashtapadis focus on shringara, and this emotion of happiness shown by Radha as she tells her Sakhi her memories was perfectly captured by Sharayu. The composition was performed in Shudhasarang Raag and Mishra Chapu Thaalaam.

A perfect way to end a Bharatanatyam recital is by performing a Thillana. The evergreen composition of Lalgudi Jayaraman was performed by Sharayu truly bringing the same spirit and Laya of the thillana. The thillana was the Mohanakalyani Thillana in Adi Thaalam, and following it was a Mahishasurmardini strotram and the Mangalam.

The entire margam was a great insight and a glance of Sharayu’s skill in Abhinaya and Nritya which displayed itself wonderfully through her dance and her Gurus choreography.

The Chief Guest Shri Bhushan Korgaonkar then expressed his gratitude and praised the artists. He is a multifaceted artist who has worked in literature, research and performing arts.

The show ended with a vote of thanks by Guru Nisha Gilbert and Sharayu both expressing their gratitude. It was truly a magical evening and a wonderful way to celebrate an artist.
