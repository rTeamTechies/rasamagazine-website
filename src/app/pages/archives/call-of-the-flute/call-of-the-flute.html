Call of the Flute: When the melody pulls you in

What a beautiful and intimate evening to have experienced on 22nd March, 2025!

It was my first time at this venue and I was so surprised at the quality of the atmosphere I was in. The G5a Warehouse serves as a platform to arts and preserving the arts. Today was a beautiful performance, which was a collaboration between two different art forms, one of the painting styles, and one of the dance styles as well as one of the music styles.

The collaboration revolved around a common theme of Vaishnavism, which is seen in the art style of Pichwai, which is done on textiles and the dance art form of Kuchipudi, which originated to tell stories and dance dramas from the village of Kuchipudi in Andhra Pradesh. The collaboration was tied with the beautiful symphony brought by the flute, which is the most important element of Krishna and his wonderful stories told.

The event started with a beautiful performance by Hrishikesh Majumdar, who is a excellent flautist who played a Pahadi opening the performance.

The Kuchipudi performance started with a Raas, which is very important to Krishna’s stories surrounding him, and the Gopika and Radha. Amrita Lahari beautifully brought out the emotion of Raasleela that occurs when Krishna plays his flute and dances joyfully with their Gopis.

Following the first performance was a Jugalbandi between the flute and the dance surrounding the theme of Holi. This performance was very similar to Shuddha Nrittam performed in Bharatanatyam as well as in Kathak, where in the percussionist will play a beat and the dancer will follow ending the performance with a harmony of the two, both the artist beautifully showing their arts in an exceptional way.

Following this was the Tarangam, which is the highlight of a Kuchipudi repertoire, where in the dancer dances on a brass plate. The Tarangam was a classic Balkrishna Tarangam telling stories about Lord Krishna, eating the mud, then stealing butter and curd from the pots, troubling all the women as well, as playing with his friends. Amrita’s performance, really really brought out the absolute essence of Kuchipudi with fast and quick paced moments, the performance was absolutely mindblowing.

The Kuchipudi performance ended with a beautiful Thillana in Desh. In between the performances, artist, Shan Bhatnagar, was connecting the dots of Pichwai with Kuchipudi, while elaborating the different aspects of the atmosphere and the temples as well as the celebrations that happen in the temple of Lord Srinathji. His wonderful explanation about the different Shringaras that are done to beautify Lord Srinathji, the food that is served, the different rituals that are done and most important, the importance of this painting art formed in the temple as well as in art in general.

At the same time, there was a melodious rhythm and music that was coming from the flautist. Hrishikesh Majumdar, who was playing different melodies in between to engage the audience. He comes from a very musical family with his father being a legendary flautist himself.

Before the last performance, the three came together to talk to keep up with the name of the session as, Baithak where the three talked about how art draws you in. Amrita as well as Shan shared that they are not from a family of dance or music or art. Rather, it was what different experiences of their life that pulled them in. Amrita shared that she was a Bengali and Shan was not as devotional or a spiritual as he was. The message of art adopting you and you adopting art was conveyed with a strong emotion by the three of them1.

Amrita ended the performance with a Desh Thillana. The Thillana was modified to have some experiment element in it where in the original Sahityam of the Thillana, which praises Lord Shiva, as Nataraja was changed to a Bengali poem where in the Nayika, who is symbolised as mother Earth herself is calling upon the ‘dark one’ who symbolises Krishna as well as the dark rainy clouds to come and embrace her with compassion. Whilst the Thillana performed was not a traditional one, it was really exciting to watch.

It was a beautiful beautiful performance to have experienced, and I was really shocked by the culture and the art that the G5a warehouse because I was not expecting something like that, in the heart of Lower Parel.
