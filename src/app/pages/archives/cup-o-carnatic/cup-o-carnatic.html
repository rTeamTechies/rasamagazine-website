‘Cup O’ Carnatic’ — A beautiful blend between music, apt storytelling and magic!

What happens when a master storyteller who also happens to be one of India’s foremost, Veena Virtuosi and one of the finest Mridangam artists as well as Ghattam artist come together for an event, you get a concert that feels like a melodious conversation. That was the promise and the magic of the Cup O’ Carnatic show the live stage event of Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh’s popular web series, which is presented at The Studio Theatre at NMACC as part of the NMACC Bachpan children’s festival, which took place on July 20th 2025 at 6 PM. The show put itself as a friendly door into Carnatic music for families, small children, for the first time without dilating the complexities of Carnatic music.

To talk about Dr Jayanthi Kumaresh is never enough. She is India’s torch bearer worldwide for Saraswati Veena, which is the national instrument of India. She is an artist, a collaborator, composer, researcher, creator, pioneer, and an educator spanning her professional life for over 35 years, she truly embodies the rich musical heritage of India. Dr Jayanthi Kumaresh today is one of the legendary Veena Masters that India has ever produced. She has performed at various prestigious venues and festivals in India as well as the globe.

She has received many awards in India such “Sangeetha Choodamani”, “Kalaimamani” by the Government of Tamil Nadu, “Veena Naada Mani”, “Kala Ratna”, “Sathyashree”, “Gaana Varidhi”, is a ten-time recipient of The Music Academy, Chennai’s “Award for Veena”, “Sangeet Shikar Samman”, “Indira Sivasailam Endowment Medal”, to name a few. Today she has presented the Saraswati Veena to audiences all around the globe. She has also worked with legendary artists such as Ustad Zakir Hussain and has presented multiple duets with North South jugalbandis with several other maestros. She holds a doctorate for her works on styles and playing techniques of the Saraswati Veena.

One can say, her start in music can be traced back six generations and her musical journey started at the age of three under her mother Vidushi Lalgudi .R. Rajalakshmi. Under her Guru Vidushi Padmavathi Anandgopalan, she studied for 22 years living with her and learnt the intricate science and art of playing the Saraswati Veena. She was under the tutelage of Veena virtuoso Dr. S. Balachander, who further enhanced her study. Truly Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh has dedicated her life to playing the Saraswati Veena.

Supporting her in the show were two formidable rhythm companions K. U. Jayachandra Rao on the Mridangam while Giridhar Udupa on the Ghatam. With their expertise in percussive knowledge, and ‘Konnakol’ mattered a lot, allowing for young audiences to feel the Taala before they learn to count it.

The Cup O Carnatic show performed at NMACC was different from the web series. According to Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh, It was only the third or fourth show that occurred, calling it a ‘Baby Show’. The show started with a story of a child called Tejas and his journey to find his interest and mastering the skills of the Saraswati Veena when he discovered a new friend who is situated at the top of his antique Veena, which is the dragon.

Through the story, Tejas travelled from finding a Guru for him, finding who the magical dragon is, and what he is trying to do. On the parallel side, Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh showed her wonderful techniques of the Saraswati Veena as she played the background music, explained different concepts in Carnatic music, which may sound complex, but she portrayed it in such a way that even a lay man could understand it better than anyone else. There was an interactive session where she would play certain notes from a certain Raaga and the Carnatic music enthusiasts consisting of the adults, kids would answer what the name of the Raaga was. A dialogue was formed between the audience and the maestro.

As the story progressed about Tejas him finding who the lion is truly who becomes his companion at the end and understanding how Goddess Saraswati really gifted the Saraswati Veena as a wonderful blessing to the music of India, Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh also showed her impressive and beautiful knowledge as well as her singing skills to demonstrate different intricate concepts that exist at a very base level in Carnatic music.

At the end, the two percussion artists came out and gave their knowledge on what konnakol is in Carnatic music, and how instruments in Carnatic music converse with each other. The finale performance was a mysterious piece performed by Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh, and her supporting artists. The performance really showed the flair between the melodious instrument that is the Saraswati Veena as well as the two strong percussion instruments that is the Mridangam and the Ghattam. The finale performance ended with a fabulous jugalbandi or a question-answer segment between the Veena and the Mridangam and Ghattam.

The show was meant to allow younger generations, first timers, common people to understand what Carnatic music and the world of Saraswati Veena truly is through the story of Tejas as well as understand how music can be found anywhere only if you have an ear for it.
