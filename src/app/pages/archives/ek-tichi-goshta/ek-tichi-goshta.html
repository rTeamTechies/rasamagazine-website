Ek Tichi Goshta: When the Everyday Finds its Voice!

Finding oneself throughout the busy life that one has created can be a troublesome event, but it is not impossible. This theme was reflected in the Marathi dance musical ‘Ek Tichi Goshta’ staged on 17th August, 2025 at the Royal opera House in South Bombay, one of the most memorable cultural experiences of the season, organised by the INT Aditya Birla Centre for Performing Arts. The event brought together different aspects of arts: theatre, music, and dance in a same blend that had the audiences uploading long. The wonderful show starring the renowned choreographer and performer, Phulawa Khamkar, and her daughter Aasma Khamkar, offered a very relatable as well as touching story of rediscovery, while also showcasing the immense talent of its ensemble cast and creative team.

This team behind this wonderful play was held and led by Virajas Kulkarni and directed by him along with Suraj Parasnis. The two were approached by Phulawa Khamkar when NMACC approached her to hold an event in the month of May, 2025. The duo created the story with a cinematic sensibility, using sharp transitions, stylised stages and choreography sequences that helped the narrative as well as the set to change itself without breaking any sort of rhythm. Their vision can be seen throughout the play as the play flows, right from a chaotic start of the characters to every calm and beautiful, blissful end Nishaad Golambre is credited for the musical compositions with lyrics written by Vaibhav Hiwase. The songs written for this show include contemporary melodies with a beautiful touch of traditional Marathi musical textures, allowing the music to be produced in a way that is familiar, but at the same time as fresh beats and melodies. The songs were made in such a way that they show the life of Phulawa Khamkar’s character, and every song fits a particular scene so beautifully that you do not even understand that the scene has changed, and now there is a different setting.

The heart of the show was the choreography which was handled beautifully by Phulawa Khamkar herself. Known for her immense work in films, television, and stage, Khamkar brought her signature blend of classical precision and modern flair to the production. The dance sequences were so contemporary yet they were easy to follow, appreciate, and did not overshadow the story. For the background dancers, she recruited 12 dancers from all over Maharashtra so that the younger generation got an opportunity to experience stage and how dancing in front of a larger audience shapes one’s dance skills.

The way the choreography was created for songs was amazing. For instance the opening song, “Uth chal Dhav pal”, talked about Phulawa Khamkar’s characters transitioning from her house to the rickshaw to the train to her office and back once again, and doing that routine for almost 20 years. The dancers danced in such a way that they could move the set in a way that at one point, it resembled her house, it resembled the rickshaw, it resembled the train, and then with the use of props it resembled the office and back again to the same routine. That was the magic of the staging, the set, the choreography and the dancers all created by the wonderful team.

The centre of the story is the character ‘TI’ who is called Anaya played by Phulawa Khamkar. Anaya’s life is shown as one defined by routines, responsibilities and emotional burdens that she has laid for herself throughout 20 years of her life. She is tired and wants to break free, but doesn’t get the opportunity to do so until she encounters a stranger who is none other than Aasma Khamkar, daughter of Phulawa Khamkar, who is an exceptional dancer and has been learning under her mother since her birth. Aasma’s character, although does not have a name, helps Anaya to find herself. She helps her trace her journey from monotony to self realisation, she helps her in understanding that as she settled in life, she should live life for herself, not for her husband, not for her son, not for her parents, but for herself, her liking and the way she wants it to be.

At the very end, Anaya realises that the character of Aasma is none other than teenager Anaya, who has come back from the past to help the current Anaya find her true self who she had locked away 30 years ago.

The wonderful music, dance as well as brilliantly written dialogues, carried the narrative with each song or choreograph sequences, which showed the transition from monotony to a more colourful life in Anaya‘s journey.

The on stage bonding between the mother-daughter duo really brought out a different form of energy as well as a smile to the audience's face, knowing the relation between the two. The chemistry between the two and the real life bond helped deepen the emotional impact of the narrative.

While the musical play was a foremost theatrical event, it didn’t stop from leaving the audiences in a reflective aftertaste. The play helped remind the viewers that life’s biggest change often happens in the smallest of pauses. Life is not about running away from responsibilities, but it is about learning to inhabit them and face them differently.

The play ended on a beautiful imagery where the current Anaya embraced the younger Anaya, which helped create an image of renewed sense of self. It created a reminder that art can mirror life's constant search for balance. The production pushed forward the idea that sometimes it is okay to let go of that routine, it is okay to find and be the true self that you used to be years ago because life should be authentic and you should be true to your authentic self.

The beautiful play, Ek Tichi Goshta, was not only a performance, but also an event that celebrated different artists, it gave an opportunity to younger generations to connect with the play and also take part in it, and at the same time helped audiences to connect to the bottom line of this play is that one should always be true to oneself and live life, the way you want to be.
