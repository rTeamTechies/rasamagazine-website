An evening of Aharya by Sri Devi Nrithyalaya

Every year, NCPA has multiple dance festivals which shows different types of dances with different aesthetics under a particular theme of that festival and one such festival was the Mudra festival which happened in 2024. The theme of this particular festival was Aharya. Aharya symbolises the costuming, the jewellery and the get-up that a dancer does in order to portray a character. Usually in a traditional performance, which doesn’t come under a dance drama, the dancer plays multiple roles, but wearing the same costume. However, in a dance drama, there is an exploration of costume and get-ups for every character. Whether it is Krishna with his beautiful dark blue skin or it is Chandika in her red hue.

On the 27 April 2024, Sri Devi Nrithyalaya performed for the first half of the Mudra Festival Day 1. Since the theme of the festival was Aharya, Guru Dr. Smt. Sheela Unnikrishnan decided to show excerpts of the very popular dance drama of the school, Srinivasa Kalyanam, along with that a few invocatory pieces and the most iconic Kalinga Nartana Thillana at the end.

Sri Devi Nrithyalaya has produced eminent performers who had taken part in this festival, such as Harinie Jeevitha, Bhairavi Venkatesan, Mridula and Mrinalini Sivakumar, Sanjena Ramesh, Kameshwari Ganesan and many more. The performance began with a Sharda Kauthuvam. A Kauthuvum is a traditional short piece performed which honours a specific deity. Originally, this is a Marathi song penned by Shantabai Shelke, which was adapted to a traditional Kauthuvum format. The dancers particularly adorned white for this to resemble the white that Devi Saraswati wears and symbolises. The Sharda Kauthuvum was one which showed that how classical dance can be adapted to different kinds of music while showing the beauty of the music and the art. With a mixture of rhythmic wordings and the sahityam, the Kauthuvum beautifully showed the different attributes of the Devi. Following that was a beautiful performance of the Hanuman Chalisa, which through choreography showed strength and power resembling that of Lord Hanuman. The choreography was in such a way that it adapted to the classical dance format while also swaying with the music. It is very unusual to dance on hindi compositions but the choreography was such that it fit perfectly like a jigsaw, beautifully showcased by the dancers.

Following it with the excerpts from the dance drama, Srinivasa Kalyanam. It started with Sage Bhrigu going to all the three gods and goddesses to their abodes in order to have a conversation and pay his respects. Sage Bhrigu arrives at Brahma Loka, where Lord Brahma and his concert Saraswati are in deep talk shown through the dance, symbolising calmness, peace, and tranquility. The duo of Brahma and Saraswati dance complex patterns but showed it in the calm manner of the dieties they were portraying. Sage Bhrigu gets angry and goes away. He then approaches Kailash, where Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati are engaged in a romantic and serious dance sequence. The use of rhythmic symbols and not words, for this particular portion of the dance symbolises and showed the deepness and seriousness of the love between Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati. The dancers through technique displayed the strength yet neatness of the duo. Sage Brighu, once again gets angry and leaves the place. He then approaches Vaikuntha where Lord Vishnu is engrossed in a romantic yet playful time with his wife. Lakshmi. The dance sequence opens with a beautiful use of lighting to show Vaikuntha with the dancers, seated on a bench in the form of Vishnu and Lakshmi. The sequence between the two deities is perfectly shown through the dance, setting the mood of the situation, right before the entry of Sage Bhrigu. The Sage arrives, but he’s paid no attention by Vishnu nor Mahalakshmi.

Getting angry, the Sage kicks Vishnu and that catches his attention. Vishnu immediately bows down to the sage and tells him to take his seat. Goddess Lakshmi gets angry and through the dance, expresses her anger after Sage Bhrigu leaves, and her discomfort, her feeling of being and turned impure after her husband getting kicked in the chest where she is believed to reside. This particular dance sequence shows the confusion, chaos as well as the anger that Goddess Lakshmi has, which then creates problems for Vishnu as Goddess Lakshmi leaves. That was the end of the excerpts from Srinivasa Kalyana. The three excerpts shown from the Srinivasa Kalyanam was shown in such a beautiful way which told the story without having to explain much. Srinivasa Kalyanam is one of the most beloved dance dramas that the dance school has produced and has performed over 30 shows.

The last and concluding performance from Sri Devi Nrithyalaya was the evergreen Kalinga Nartana Thillana with Harinie Jeevitha as Krishna herself. The entire Thillana is a playful choreography of Krishna, defeating the serpent Kalinga and dancing on his head in a melodious, joyous way to show his victory and the beauty of Krishna. The Thillana, can be considered as one of the most playful and brillint choroegraphies of Dr. Sheela Unnikrishnan. With intricate formations and adavus, the performance shows the playful rasa of the Thillana.

The performance itself was a treat to the eyes. Such beauty and precision as well as complex choreography shown through different performances, which fall under the theme of Aharya were showcased. Sri Devi Nrithyalaya has always been perfect in terms of dance, the quality of the dance as well as respect to the artform.
