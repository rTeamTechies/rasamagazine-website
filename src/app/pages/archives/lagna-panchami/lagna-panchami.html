Chaos, comedy and confusion in a play!

A play that reflects every married couple's nightmare but through the lens of comedy.

Photo credits - Mayrangabhumi

In Marathi culture, theatre has always been a prominent medium for information dissemination and entertainment. Marathi plays have always created a name for themselves by shedding light on social topics, friendship, religious ideologies, beliefs, and much more. As a Marathi person myself, I admit that lately I have not visited or experienced a Marathi play in recent times, but I was fortunate enough to find one play, which made me really excited to watch it.

For me, entertainment is the best source of escapism. Any film, TV series, or play that deals with comedy-drama and much more is something that is always fascinating to me and one of such plays was Lagna Panchami directed by the director Nipun Dharmadhikari, written by Madhugandha Kulkarni. I discovered this play on BookMyShow and I was going to go for their performance at NCPA, but unfortunately, I was too late to book the tickets, but I found their show at the Mahakavi Kalidas Auditorium in Mulund, and I was just blown away by the level of comedy that I witnessed that day.

Starring Swapnil Joshi, Amruta Khanvilkar, Madhugandha Kulkarni and Sunil Abhyankar, this play is a pure comedy, but it also deals with certain aspects of family life and also the bond that creates and/or breaks the relation between a husband and a wife.

Photo credits - Loksatta

Swapnil Joshi‘s character is called Satvik while Amrita Khanvilkar’s character is called Suryaja. The two, husband and wife, are living a very fast paced yet opposite lives where Satvik is a writer for daily soaps while Suryaja works at a bank. The two are newly married with a new house on their name, a new car on their name and starting a new life. The play starts with Suryaja coming home to find her husband dead on the sofa. She very comically prays for him to come back and at that moment, the God of Death,Yamraj, comes to take her husband away. She pleads to the Lord that instead of him take her away, but the Lord says that it is not written according to her faith. Suryaja puts on a dramatic yet comical display of emotions, crying like a typical housewife for her “Pati Parmeshwar”. Frustrated Yamraj then tells her that he will not take her husband right now, but he realises that the two of them, although newly married, are having lots of problems in their marriage. The God of Death gives them time till 1st January to sort out the mess of their marriage, but he gives them one condition that if they are able to sustain their marriage post 1st January, then he will not take anyone, but if they get divorced or separate each other, then he will take one of them to hell.

From there, begins the chaos of the couple trying to live their life normally. They forget about this Yamraj incident and go back to living normal lives. A series of events happen in such a manner that both of them leave their job, making it a difficult time for both of them to sustain with a new house on their name, a new car on their name and a lot more bills to come. The banter between the husband and wife was such that it was something that every person in the auditorium, who is either married or not married, could relate to. Whether it is keeping the house clean or putting the laundry away properly or not interfering in other people's business, it is something that has been experienced by everyone. The audience consisted of a lot of married couples and a lot of children like myself. Their parents fight like this, which is normal because what is a relationship without a fight?

Soon enough, the two of them create a mutual understanding and try to get their job back by pleading to their old bosses. But in this process of getting the job back, getting back to earning, it leads to both of them creating a distance between themselves. A major twist in the story comes when Suryaja reveals that her mother had faced this problem. Suryaja‘s father had started cheating on her mother at a very young age, but her mother never left the marriage. Instead, she lived and she created a wall in the house by using a saree and that wall stayed till the end. Suryaja in her mind didn’t want this to happen to herself and her marriage. For her, it was developing that sense of heroic nature where if something like this did happen, then she would divorce her husband and not live a life like her mother. Her suspicions about her husband cheating, also come to fruition when Satvik‘s old girlfriend starts to call him. His girlfriend is very nosy, and she’s trying very hard to get close to Satvik. Satvik knows that he loves Suryaja and not his old girlfriend. All of these rapid suspicions come to a blast when the two of them have an enormous fight, leaving both of them into separate parts of their houses.

In the second act of the play, the play opens in a very comical manner. I had not laughed this much in so long that it made me super happy to have taken this decision to come to this play. Both of the characters of Satvik and Suryaja go to two different people. Satvik goes to a marriage counsellor played by Madhugandha Kulkarni and Suryaja goes to a divorce lawyer played by Sunil Abhyankar. Satvik is trying to learn how to be calm and how to be a good husband for his wife while on the other hand, Suryaja is at a divorce lawyer to try to understand how to capture moments where her husband is being mean to her, and it is the best scene according to me in the entire play because the comedy back-and-forth between the four actors is just impeccable and the jokes written are such that they are so seamless that it doesn’t seem forced at all.

After this very comic intersection, the two husband and wife come home and try to do their job while the husband is trying to be a good husband. The wife is mad because she cannot find any evidence of his careless and mean behaviour. This leads to an even more chaotic and comic rumble. But as always when silence is prevalent, it means that there is a storm coming and soon enough, there is a massive fight between the two husband and wife, Satvik, and Suryaja, leading to a long wall between the two of them.

The couple are visited by Satvik’s mother and father played by the same actors who played the marriage counsellor and the lawyer. These two particular scenes where Satvik has a conversation with his father, and Suryaja has another conversation with Satvik‘s mother shows the calm and understanding path that needs to be established in a marriage. The dialogues emphasise that there needs to be a mutual understanding of give-and-take and understanding one another in different situations, especially for newly married couples.

At the very end of the play, Satvik leaves the house with his bags and leaves a note for his wife along with divorce papers right on the date of first January. Suryaja comes home to an empty house and notices the divorce papers and the note written by her husband. First, She seems to agree to the divorce but something changes and she calls her husband back to her house to apologise to each other, forgive each other and cry in moments of happiness and sadness, and that’s when Yamraj appears there immediately. The couple immediately remember the date and the importance of the date and what was going to happen, Yamraj tricks them and tells them that he will take Suryaja for an instance but is immediately brought to life because Yamraj reveals that he is just joking with him, and in fact reveals that Suryaja is pregnant. He wishes the couple a happy life, a happy marriage, and a happy parenthood on and then exits. And the play comes to an end.

It was a very refreshing piece of content for me to see. First of all, it has been a very long time since I have seen any theatrical performance or play that too, in Marathi. And I am very glad that I took this decision to go and watch it, and I thoroughly enjoyed it most probably because it was comedy through and through, although there was a lot of emphasis on societal roles and emotional well-being, but when all of it is put through a comical lens, it’s all fun.

I was particularly impressed by the space of the Mahakavi Kalidas Auditorium in Mulund because it was a huge theatre, but it was not just a theatre, it’s a sports complex and just to add a note, I think spaces like these are very important in today’s metropolitan cities.

The play was such that it dealt with something so common, so relatable and so every day that everyone and the audience understood it in a matter of seconds. For them, it was not just a play, but it felt as if their own lives were being put on as a show, because marriages happen and fights happen. There is fighting in every relationship, whether it is with your parents, with your friends or with your loved one, and this play puts an exact emphasis on this that we need to get away from these fights and treat them like cracks in walls. We need to put cement in these cracks and focus on building the wall
