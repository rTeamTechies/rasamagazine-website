Living the Dance: An enigmatic performance by the Nrityagram ensemble!

On the 30th of January, I had the wonderful honor of attending the Spectrum Festival, which is held annually by the National Center for Performing Arts, NCPA. It is a festival where different talented artists perform and show the audiences of Mumbai their talent, their artistry, their skills, giving an opportunity to all Mumbai audiences to appreciate them in a larger way. The Spectrum Festival has been going on for many years. It celebrates the diverse colours of Indian classical dance and brings together established practitioners as well as emerging voices on one stage. It is a wonderful platform created by NCPA for Mumbai audiences to see the talent that is flowering not only in Mumbai but all over the world.

For Spectrum Day One, there were two performances lined up by established, esteemed performers. The first half of the evening was taken by Rama Vaidyanathan and her students, coming all the way from Delhi. And the second performance, which really blew my mind and something that I was looking forward to, was the performance by the Nrityagram ensemble coming from Bengaluru. Nrityagram is located in a quiet corner of Bengaluru, surrounded by red earth, trees as well as an atmosphere which is far away from the bustle of the city. Nrityagram literally means ‘Dance Village’. And it was born from the extraordinary vision of Protima Gauri, who wanted to create a community where artists could devote themselves entirely to the craft of dance. And today, this institution and this place stands as one of India's most distinctive institutions for classical dance and particularly for Odissi. Protima Gauri envisioned a sanctuary and a sacred space where dancers could withdraw from the distractions and disturbances of everyday life and could come together to pour their heart into complete learning, practicing and understanding whatever art form they wanted to practice. And it was this vision that took form in the physical world outside Bengaluru. What began as an isolated piece of land became a gurukula eventually, a residential community in which teachers, students live, learn and work together, also reigniting the ancient guru-shishya parampara.

And one such student of Protima Gauri at Nrityagram who has completely transformed the world of Odissi through her choreography is Surupa Sen. Surupa completed her degree in economics and arrived at Nrityagram intending to learn Odissi only for a short period of time. However, she found herself entering a six-year residential program and eventually started building a life around dance. And now more than three decades later, she stands as one of the most influential and contemporary voices in the world of Odissi, both as a dancer as well as a choreographer.

Surupa Sen was the first student to graduate from Nrityagram's program. Her training brought her into contact with some of the most important figures of Indian classical dance. She studied Odissi with Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra, Protima Gauri, Ratikant Mohapatra and Bichitrananda Swain and while also studied Abhinaya from the Abhinaya maestro, Kalanidhi Narayanan and also learned Natya Shastra under Dr. Padma Subramanyam. These influences have given Surupa a strong and formidable foundation in tradition and it can be reflected through her artistic journey, which has been nothing but extraordinary. The most defining contribution of Surupa Sen to Odissi has always been her choreography. She does not treat classical dance as just fixed vocabulary, but she is constantly questioning how movement is created and can change, how space and rhythm move along with movement and how all of these elements interact with music and gesture. Her research over decades has expanded the vocabulary of Odissi and has also contributed to the aesthetic that has become closely associated with the Nrityagram style.

Since 2000, Surupa has choreographed Nrityagram's repertoire and also created full-length ensembles, duets and solo works. One of her notable productions is Sri: In Search of the Goddess. It is a dance production which explores the feminine principle through the vocabulary of Odissi dance, while also moving beyond conventional representation of the goddess towards an exploration of how feminine energy and consciousness work. Music is equally important to Surupa Sen's choreographic language. In Surupa Sen's choreography, rhythm, melody, design, spatiality and the physicality of the dancer come together as different parts of one larger artistic identity. She has been awarded and recognized with several honors for her contribution to the world of Odissi, such as the Raza Foundation Award in 2006, the Yagnaraman Award in 2008, Nritya Choodamani in 2011 and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award for Odissi Dance in 2018. She has also presented her work at major festivals and venues in India as well as internationally. As artistic director of Nrityagram, she has helped shape an institution that does not choose between preservation and innovation, rather encourages both. Her work and her style demonstrates that respecting a classical art form does not mean leaving it untouched. However, tradition itself can be a foundation from which new artistic possibilities emerge. In Surupa's Odissi, movement is more than just movement. It is curiousness, it is investigation of the body, of space, of rhythm and ultimately of what dance can still become.

All of these wonderful elements that exist in the world of Odissi created by Surupa Sen were quite evident at Nrityagram's performance for the Spectrum Festival. The dancers who performed were Anoushka Rahman, Pavithra Reddy, Namaha Mazoomdar, Daquil Miriyala and Surupa Sen. The performance was a display of devotion, years of dedication, passion and discipline that have been put forward by these dancers for their love of Odissi.

The performance started with ‘Sankirtanam’, which is a prayer, described as wandering minstrels singing and dancing in praise of Lord Krishna. This was a very beautiful and enchanting piece. The music was quite strong, forceful and powerful, which complemented the beautiful movements of Odissi. This was a wonderful start and a beautiful homage to the deity that exists in Odissi, Lord Jagannath. Following Sankirtanam was a Gita Govind performed solo by Surupa Sen, titled ‘Pashyati Dishi Dishi’. This was a true Abhinaya piece, beautifully, beautifully performed by Surupa ji. Radha is on her way through the midst of the forest to meet Krishna. She wanders through this mysterious forest of Vrindavana to meet her lover at the very stroke of midnight. She encounters multiple things that obstruct her, but eventually she finally goes into the embrace of Krishna. And such beautiful Abhinaya was translated into real life through the movements created by Surupa Sen. Surupa Sen's facial expressions have always been quite charismatic and she is able to pull in the audiences through subtle expressions and her movement of the body while making sure that the audiences are knowing and understanding what is going on, which is a very quintessential thing that a dancer should be able to do, which is create the atmosphere of the composition in a very seamless, beautiful and step-by-step manner. And Surupa Sen's choreography and performance brought exactly that. She had wonderfully portrayed the mysterious forest, a curious Radha going through the forest, eventually letting go of her anger and meeting her Krishna.

Following this Gita Govind was the central piece of the evening, titled ‘Aakriti’, described as an abstract dance. This was performed by Pavithra Reddy, Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala, Namaha Mazoomdar. Aakriti, I think, was the best performance of the entire evening. The performance focused on displaying the sculptural postures of Odissi through rhythm and movement, but it was not forced in any manner. The movements by the dancers were so seamless, were so beautiful and so elegant, as they showed the beauty of Odissi and the strength of Odissi as well. All the four dancers, Pavithra Reddy, Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala and Namaha Mazoomdar, were able to keep up with such stamina throughout the performance, because Odissi is a very demanding dance art form. It requires a lot of physical strength and I was just in awe of how complex movements were being performed with such grace, such elegance and without any effort. That was Aakriti.

Following Aakriti was a dance in praise of the Mother Goddess, called ‘Sri Devi’. This was a beautiful end to this wonderful and enigmatic presentation by Nrityagram Ensemble for the Spectrum Festival. This was performed by Pavithra Reddy, Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala and Namaha Mazoomdar. The composition and the performance itself was an ode to the Mother Goddess and all the four dancers were able to show the different aspects of the Mother Goddess, whether it is maternal care, whether it is grace, whether it is the anger of vanquishing the egos of different demons, all these wonderful aspects were brought out through this wonderful performance. And post Sri Devi was a small excerpt taken from the Mahishasuramardini Stotram, concluding this wonderful performance.

These four pieces that were performed by the Nrityagram Ensemble were just so charismatic, so wonderful to watch. I was just sitting in awe and in complete silence, taking all the beauty, taking all the artistry that is being displayed on the stage and just pondering on how many years of devotion, dedication and discipline must have been gone in order to achieve this sense of seamlessness, effortlessness, grace and talent. I was thoroughly amazed by this performance and this has just left me wanting to watch more of Nrityagram performances in the future.

One important thing about this performance was that it was accompanied by live musicians. The music compositions were done by Pandit Raghunath Panigrahi and for the Gita Govind - Pashyati Dishi Dishi, it was done by Pandit Bhubaneswar Mishra. The musicians that left everyone amazed through their skills equally along with the performers were Jatin Sahu, lead vocal and harmonium, Rohan Dahale on chants and mardala, Parshuram Das on the bamboo flute, Anup Kulthe on the violin and for the later performances, Manjira was done by Surupa Sen.

Spectrum Festival Day 1 was concluded by a wonderful performance by Nrityagram and I can thoroughly and very surely say that Nrityagram is truly one of my favorite dance troupes, dance schools and institutions that has been doing wonderful things for the world of arts today. It is very amusing to find a space like Nrityagram exists even today and it just shows how dedicated Surupa Sen and all the dancers and performers and the residents of Nrityagram are to making sure that the legacy and the heritage, the traditions, the culture of our Indian classical arts stays alive, vibrant. And this was very much evident through this wonderful performance. And I can't wait to see more performances by Nrityagram in the future.
