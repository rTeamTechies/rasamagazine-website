Sridevi Nrithyalaya’s Madhavam Mahadevam was not just a performance, it was a heavenly experience!

On the 26th of Jan 2025, Sridevi Nrithyalaya came to perform in Mumbai at the Chembur Fine Arts Society in Chembur for the Indradhanush Festival, which is an annual festival held by the Chembur Fine Arts Society. I was so excited to finally see Sridevi Nrithyalaya after so long after the performance in April 2024. I was very excited to see what Sridevi Nrithyalaya was going to bring and I was, as always, taken aback.

Sridevi Nrithyalaya is a wonderful dance school in Chennai, which was started by Dr. Sheela Unnikrishnan, who is a distinguished Bharatanatyam Guru, choreographer and teacher, whose artistic journey has been deeply shaped and rooted in the Melattur style of Bharatanatyam. She started learning the art form at the age of 11 under her Guru, Railway Shri Sundaram, who was a senior disciple of Mangudi Sri Dorairaja Iyer and she also underwent intensive training in Kuchipudi under the legendary Dr. Vempati Chinnasatyam.

In 1987, she founded the dance school Sridevi Nrithyalaya and began with a small group of students in Villivakkam, Chennai. She had immense dedication to teaching choreography as well as nurturing the young dancers, who have transformed the institution into an acclaimed center for Bharatanatyam. She was awarded a doctorate by the Tamil University, Thanjavur, in 2017 for her research on dance aspects in Sittrilakkiyam. Sridevi Nrithyalaya stands as one of the foremost dance schools in India for Bharatanatyam today. It specializes in the Melattur style and is a dance space which puts a strong emphasis on individual attention, rigorous practice and holistic development of each student. There is no uniform syllabus that is followed. The progress of students is assessed according to every dancer's ability, dedication and growth within the art form. The remarkable caliber of her students stands as the finest testament of Dr. Sheela Unnikrishnan's teaching. Four students of the institution have received the prestigious Balashree Award, while others have won multiple scholarships, national and international competition titles and have received major recognition from sabhas. Students such as Harinie Jeevitha, Bhairavi Venkateshan, Mridula and Mrinalini Sivakumar, Sanjana Ramesh, Kameshwari Ganesan, have all established themselves as accomplished dancers and through her students, Dr. Sheela continues to display how disciplined training, artistic imagination, as well as the importance of Guru-shishya bond can sustain and carry forward the legacy of Bharatanatyam.

And all of these wonderful aspects of discipline, dedication, as well as love for the art form were quite visible in the performance that they held on 26th January. Dr. Sheela introduced the evening as a thematic presentation called Madhavam Mahadevam, which was an ode to the two Gods, Lord Vishnu and Lord Mahadeva, the counterpart and better halves of each other, in a way. The performances were taken as such to honour and pay obeisance to the two gods. In a very classic Margam style, the performance began with a Chappumalika Alarippu. It was so wonderful to see the students of Sridevi Nrithyalaya perform and not just students, but acclaimed performers as well. All of these performers have created a name for them. The Chappumalika Alarippu was performed by Bhairavi Venkateshan, Harinie Jeevitha, Kameshwari Ganesan, Mridula Sivakumar and Mrinalini Sivakumar. The five of them displayed the very quintessential essence of Melattur bani within the Nritta that Alarippu has.

Following the Alarippu was the Natakurinji Varnam. It is a very classic choreography by Dr. Sheela, which pays obeisance to Lord Shiva as he saves Markandeya from the clutches of death, from Yamaraja. This composition of Natakurinji Varnam by Papanasan Sivam is a classic choreography and it was so wonderful to see this choreography be performed live as someone who has watched it over and over on YouTube and to finally see all of them perform this wonderful composition displaying the wonderful aspects of dance. Sridevi Nrithyalaya and all of the students under this dance school have immense talent and artistry, which was completely visible through the performance by Bhairavi, Harinie, Mridula, Mrinalini and Kameshwari. The varnam had beautiful sancharis, which complemented the disciplined and distinct architecture of the nritta that was used. The jathis were crisp, containing and complemented the expressions of the dancers.

Following the Natakurinji Varnam was a beautiful padam performed by Sanjana Ramesh, Mridula Sivakumar and Bhairavi Venkateshan. The three of them performed ‘Taye Yashoda’, a very sweet and fun padam where a gopi, played by Mridula Sivakumar, complains to Yashoda, played by Sanjana Ramesh, about Krishna, played by Bhairavi Venkateshan, about all the pranks and tricks played by Krishna. The padam was quite fun as the tricks of Krishna, which were being explained by Mridula in a very fun manner, brought laughs and smiles to the faces of the audiences. And at the end, Yashoda could not say anything to her darling child and told the gopi to go away, a classic end to a classic Krishna padam.

Following this padam was a traditional Melattur performance titled Shiva Tandava Padam, where Mridula and Mrinalini Sivakumar danced brilliantly, showing the atmosphere and Kailasa, where the Lord of dance and the Goddess of dance come together to perform in a melodious harmony, both showing equally wonderful skills of dance. Mridula played Goddess Parvati during the ekaharya and Mrinalini played Lord Shiva within the ekaharya as well. The two of them performed in such a manner that it almost felt as if we were seeing the Lord and the Goddess perform at Kailasa as they took joy in creating a sort of jugalbandhi between each other. This composition and performance stands as one of my favorites even today, as I really, really love the Shivtandava Padam.

The end of the performance happened with a fantastic and exhilarating performance of the Dashavatar Ashtapadi, Pralaya Payodhi Jale by Jayadeva. This originally was performed by Meghna Unnikrishnan, another student of Guru Dr. Sheela Unnikrishnan, for her Arangetram, where she combined the aspect of drawing and painting along with dancing. So as she danced every verse praising each avatar of Lord Vishnu, she would draw the avatar, creating a wonderful painting at the end of the performance. And it was so beautiful and everyone in the audience was on their toes as soon as this piece was announced as the last and final piece. Everyone whipped out their phone immediately to capture this wonderful performance. The Pralaya Payodhi Jale Ashtapadi was performed by Bhairavi Venkatesan, Harinie Jeevitha, Mridula and Mrinalini Sivakumar and Kameshwari Ganesan, as they each took turns in painting the wonderful depiction of the Dashavatar, while performing and dancing at the same time. Each of the verses brought out the beautiful story of all the ten avatars of Lord Vishnu and they were equally reflected in a beautiful manner on the canvas, with every brush stroke being painted.

It was a dream come true to watch Sridevi Nrithyalaya perform all these classic choreographies of their style in Mumbai for the Mumbai audiences. I am deeply grateful to have gotten the chance to experience the wonderful performance and I can't wait to watch more, as always, of Sridevi Nrithyalaya's performance here in Mumbai.
