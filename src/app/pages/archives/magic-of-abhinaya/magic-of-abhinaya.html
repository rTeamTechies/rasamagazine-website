The magic of Abhinaya, never witnessed before.

A magical evening turned into a masterclass on Abhinaya by the great Doyenne of Abhinaya herself!

Photo credits - Indian Express

What happens when the Doyenne of Abhinaya herself comes and puts on a performance that cannot be expressed in words? That’s exactly what happened on 17th April at The Studio Theatre at NMACC. Priyadarshini Govind, along with her ensemble, put on a show that can only be expressed in applause and compliments and nothing else. She brought in a performance called Rasa Manjari, an exploration of different rasas.

Priyadarshini Govind is one of the foremost exponents as well as renowned Bharatanatyam dancers of contemporary India. She is particularly known for her Abhinaya, expressive depth as well as strong traditions and nuances. She trained under Guru Swamimalai K. Rajaratnam of the Vazhuvoor school for Nritya and learnt and trained under the legendary Guru Kalanidhi Narayanan. Priyadarshini Govind is celebrated, especially for her expressive and complex Abhinaya. Due to her vast expansive repertoire, she has emerged as a leading figure in the preservation as well as advancement of the classic traditions of Bharatanatyam. Priyadarshini Govind has contributed significantly to the dance world too. She served as the director of Kalakshetra Foundation from 2013 to 2017 and expanded her career to multiple avenues, multiple accolades and praises of fame. She has received the Kalaimamani from the Tamil Nadu State Government as well as the Sangeet Natak Akademi award. She is celebrated for performances of pieces like Krishna Ne Begane Baaro, as well as exploring different Nayïka emotions. Truly, she is a gem in the world of Bharatanatyam.

Priyadarshini Govind was joined by a stunning and talented group of musicians. She started the performance with the traditional Thodayam, an evergreen invocatory piece. The first piece brought out the traditional aspects of the dance. With a lot of precision and sharp movements, Priyadarshini Govind executed the performance beautifully.

Photo credits - Sarvani Yadavalli

Following the invocatory piece was the main and central piece of the evening called as Ravan. This is one of the most celebrated pieces of Priyadarshini Govind’s repertoire. She introduced the piece as an introduction to the invincible King of Lanka. Ravan was a devoted follower and an ardent devotee of Lord Shiva, who was ready to sacrifice himself to get a boon from Lord Brahma. Lord Brahma gave him a boon that only a mortal man can kill him. Ravan was so consumed with power, he was sure that no mortal can come as close as to him. He grew a lot of pride for himself and self declared himself King of Lanka. At the same time, however, he was a righteous king who made sure that his subjects lived a happy life, but one mistake left him crumbling, ultimately leading to his death and that was kidnapping a married woman and lusting over her. Priyadarshini Govind began her performance with a powerful entry. The music and the lighting added to the composition to show the power as well as the strength of the great invincible King of Lanka. He was shown to have the eight Siddhis, which are powers which allow one to transfer into a tiny being, to a giant, to swiftly go across destinations and to control the five elements. Priyadarshini Govind showed these powers of Ravan through a sanchari. The performance involved recurring jathis, which added that traditional aspect to the entire performance. She very marvellously and through her expressive abhinaya showed the consummation of power that Ravan goes through after gaining the boon from Lord Brahma. Through her sancharis, she showed how he was ready to sacrifice his heads and himself to please Lord Brahma. The second half of the piece focused on a statement by Lord Rama. Lord Rama says to Ravan during the battle “Go back and come tomorrow”, which signified Lord Rama allowing Ravan to live a righteous life by repenting over his mistake. Priyadarshini Govind showed this one particular line through the 10 heads of Ravan. Each head showed a different emotion. Some heads showed shock with the mouth agape, while some showed fear, some showed as terrified, some as looking and confused to the ground and the sky. This was the climax of the performance and showed the abhinaya of the artist in the most splendid and excellent way.

Following the brilliant piece was a very calming, soothing and a small piece called Gaja Shringara. A piece that I have never seen and I don’t think anyone has performed in a traditional Bharatanatyam performance. A very small poem beautifully and very happily shown through the dance by Priyadarshini Govind. The piece talks about two elephants, a female and a male elephant. The male elephant goes to war and while fighting, he breaks one of his tusks and his toenails get chipped as he is trying to crush the heads of the enemy kings. He comes back from the war, but he’s very afraid to go in front of his companion as he thinks that due to these imperfections, he is now not suited for her, but on the contrary, his female companion applauds him and finds it as a sense of bravery when she sees these imperfections as well as battle scars of victory. A very beautiful, small and peaceful piece.

After Gaja Shringara, was a Bhajan by Tulsidas called Thumaka Chalat Rama. It is a piece which talks about baby Rama. Priyadarshini Govind, through her expressive character of Kaushalya, the mother of Lord Rama, shows the two playing and getting Rama ready for the day while admiring and cooing her baby. The portrayal of a mother felt really realistic, and it didn’t seem as if it was a performance or an acting. It felt very natural and felt very real. That is the magic of Priyadarshini Govind’s abhinaya.

The concluding piece was an abhanga by Sant Bhanudas called Vrindavani Venu. A magical end to the evening by Priyadarshini Govind. The Abhanga talks about the atmosphere of Vrindavan when Krishna plays the magical flute, bringing in that magic of peace, calmness, and happiness to everyone there and such was the reflection through the dance of Smt. Priyadarshini Govind. A beautiful end to a beautiful performance indeed.

This was my first time watching Priyadarshini Govind live, although I have been keeping up with her work for a very long time, but it was really beautiful and surreal to meet her in person and take a picture with her at the very end to express my gratitude for her to come to Mumbai to show her talent and excellence, and give a chance to the Mumbai audiences to witness her talent, her artistry, and her abhinaya. This performance indeed was a statement to the fact that she is definitely the Doyenne of Abhinaya in Bharatanatyam.
