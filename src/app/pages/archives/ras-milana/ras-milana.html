Ras Milana - The coming together of two art forms.

When Odissi and Kathak blend like two rivers meeting at the sea.

The evening of 19th April, 2026 at The Cube in NMACC, turned into a magical space when the two distinct and beautiful art forms of Odissi and Kathak came together in a spellbinding performance of Ras Yukti - a duet by Preetisha Mohapatra and Nilopa Maitra. Odissi and Kathak are two different styles yet they are very similar due to the usage of Hindustani music in both. Although they are similar, yet, they are very different with one focusing on postures and one focusing on the rhythm. The two dancers representing both the forms were Preetisha Mohapatra, as well as Nilopa Maitra.

Preetisha Mohapatra comes from the prestigious Mohapatra family and is the third generation dancer under her father and mother, Ratikant and Sujata Mohapatra and her grandfather, the father of Odissi, Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra. From a very young age, Preetisha has been training in this art form due to the exposure to the artform since her birth. She has learnt her grandfather’s traditional techniques, but is moulded by her father’s nuanced choreographies. Preetisha has performed at multiple venues and has been awarded multiple accolades across her career. She has performed with her mother as well as at different places showing the beauty of Odissi worldwide.

Photo credits - The Hindu

Nilopa Maitra is the daughter of her Guru Paramamitra Maitra. She has learnt Kathak under her mother and has performed at multiple venues with multiple accolades showing the beauty of Kathak at the global stage. She is the recipient of the national scholarship in Kathak from the Ministry of Culture, Government of India, and has been dancing since a very young age.

The two dancers brought in their distinctive style of dancing to this wonderful evening. The performance started with two compositions performed after the other without any break. The first one was Ardhanareeshwara, a classic choreography by Ratikant Mohapatra, which was performed by both the dancers. The original composition was up to 15 to 20 minutes, but the composition was cut short at the very first charana and was followed by a Bhajan called Aradhang but it was more fitting to the Kathak style compared to the first piece which was fitting to the Odissi style. The two dancers adorned white costumes to start the performance. One could definitely see the difference between the setting of the compositions, which fit two different styles, Odissi and Kathak.

Following the first performance was a solo performance by Preetisha Mohapatra. This was a very exciting performance as certain clips at different venues had gone viral on Instagram, especially of Preetisha and her mother, Sujata Ji. The piece was not introduced with a formal name, but can be called as Varsha. The composition talked about the importance of rain to mankind, to animals and the environment, but at the same time, how too much rain can lead to destruction. Preetisha’s portrayal of the animals as well as the most important, the beautiful peacock was spellbinding. She showcased the peacock in its natural habitat, dancing joyously, and cleaning itself while the rain falls. The composition made use of peacock noises to compliment the dance. As the composition went further, the destruction and the sadness in emotion was aptly shown by Preetisha. Her abhinaya showed the confusion, feeling of terror and fear while thinking about the destruction caused by over rainfall. This composition really makes you think about our nature. From my experience from the last performance that I attended of the students of Ratikant Mohapatra for the Spectrum Festival as well as the Nrityagurukal performance, that Ratikant Ji, puts a lot of focus on a lot of compositions which talk about environment, social causes, which is very refreshing to see in the art world today.

Up Next was a duet performance by the two dancers. It was titled as Umang. This performance was a combination of two bhajans on Krishna playing his leela during Holi with Radha. The first bhajan focused on Radha being upset that she was not the first one to put colour on Krishna and the second seamlessly followed in where Krishna joyously, along with Radha, plays Holi in Vrindavan. Preetisha played the role of Krishna in both the bhajans while Nilopa played the role of Radha. This was another performance where you could see how both the styles had adapted to the music with the posture based performance and movements by Preetisha, whereas more of flowy and circular movements by Nilopa. Both the dancers, through their abhinaya, showcased their characters very realistically, almost making it seem as if Krishna and Radha were totally playing Holi on the stage!

After Umang was a solo by Nilopa Maitra called Raah, which talked about finding one’s path in life. The concept was created by her with choreography by her mother Paramitra Ji and voice over performance was done by her. It was an abstract performance which talked about how some people are quick to tell other people about finding their role in life when they themselves are confused. The composition talked about two souls, finding their role in life while going through the joys and sadness of it. Nilopa adorned a red costume for this performance, and it started with her entering holding a lamp symbolising the light that burns during darkness. This performance brought in the traditional aspects of Kathak, but the abhinaya was more nuanced. The performance felt like a breath of fresh air.

Following this was the evergreen and iconic piece, performed by both dancers, a Tulsi Das Bhajan, Ramachandra Kripalu. This is an iconic choreography of Guru Ratikant Mohapatra, which I had witnessed during the Spectrum performance. The sanchari of the performance involved the Sita Swayamvar that happened, and the second sanchari involved the battle that happened between Ravan and Rama. Two styles but one performance and beautiful execution by both the dancers, the best part of the performance was the sanchari that showed the Sita Swayamvar.

Preetisha and Nilopa, both portrayed two characters who were overconfident kings who thought they could lift the difficult bow of Lord Shiva, but they could not. It was a very hilarious and realistic sanchari which made the entire audience smile and burst into a fit of laughter. The next sanchari that followed, showed Preetisha as Ravan and Nilopa as Rama with an intense battle scene with the ultimate killing of Ravan. The performance ended on a very high note gaining roars of applause from the audience.

The concluding piece was a Tarana. Tarana is a very symbolic and beautiful fast paced piece that is usually performed at the end of a Hindustani classical music and dance performance, usually. The Tarana was executed in a beautiful way with both the art forms flowing in like two rivers meeting at the sea. The performance ended with a standing ovation by the entire audience.

I was fortunate enough to have Paramitra Maitra Ji sat next to me, and I did not know, and I was too late to recognise and say hello. I was seated in the first row and Ratikant Mohapatra Ji was also seated, although I could not say hello, but I was lucky enough to have witnessed him there. The evening ended on a beautiful note with pictures and greetings with the artists. It was a very magical and powerful performance, which definitely talked about and displayed through the dance how the two art forms of Kathak and Odissi are so similar yet so distinct. They seem like sisters, so similar yet so different and apart.
