Vaibhav Arekar’s students honour his legacy through their own flair!

In the world of classical traditions, one very important heritage tool that needs to be protected, passed down and that needs to be promoted is the parampara of Guru-shishya traditions. The Guru-shishya relation is the backbone of any artistic community today. The trust that a Guru has in their shishya, as well as the respect the shishya has in its Gurus, leads to a formation of a relation that goes for years and years, mutually depending on each other, as well as being independent from each other as time grows. To celebrate this very wonderful Guru-shishya parampara and to help shishyas of different Gurus to gain understanding about different aspects of concert and performance management, the NCPA had conducted its Nritya Gurukul initiative in the month of March and February, where they had taken the shishyas of different Gurus of different parts of India and they had been training them over the past one year in different aspects of performance management, whether it is lighting, whether it is the finance aspect, whether it is the management of the auditorium. These are very important aspects that will help anyone in the performing arts world to get an understanding of how important it is to conduct your own presentations, either solo or group and without this knowledge, it is not possible.

The Nritya Gurukul initiative was split into two parts, with the first half of the programs being sponsored by the donor - Bank of America, while the second half was sponsored by Voltas. For the Voltas-sponsored program, there were two Gurus and five shishyas: two under the first Guru, three under the second Guru. The first Guru was Smt. Sujata Mohapatra, a leading exponent and one of the finest Odissi dancers today. And the second Guru was Vaibhav Arekar and his three shishyas who participated in the Nritya Gurukul performance. I was fortunate enough to have attended the program.

Vaibhav Arekar stands as one of the most prominent male Bharatanatyam practitioners of his generation. He is known for his approach to the classical form as intense, contemplative and deeply rooted in tradition. He was initiated into the art form by Smt. Saroja Srinath and he pursued advanced training at Nalanda Nritya Kala Mahavidyalaya, where he trained in the aspect of nritta under Smt. Thangamani Nagarajan, then received advanced training and guidance from his mentor, the late Padma Bhushan Dr. Kanak Rele. His work is quite distinguished by an interest in discovering contemporary possibilities within the classic vocabulary and he is often known to bring together different aspects of art such as dance, theatre, poetry and many thematic narrators together. He founded the Sankhya Dance Company in Pune, through which he has created and presented numerous works. Vaibhav and his students have performed in major festivals and stages, not only in India but internationally as well and his artistic practice always reflects in his students, which was quite visible in the performance of the Nritya Gurukul. Vaibhav Arekar's pedagogical work extends beyond conventional dance instruction. He has always emphasized on the importance of Guru-shishya relationship and how important it is to sustain this mentorship. His teaching goes beyond Bharatanatyam because he always gives an important focus on body conditioning and developing an individual performer's understanding of movement and expression. He has conducted many masterclasses for dance as well as body conditioning at different levels, including students from outside India as well. He does not treat dance merely as a set of movements that need to be learned, but rather encourages a pedagogy where students get to engage with artistic, expressive and intellectual dimensions of the art form. His students have gone on to become performers, teachers and core members of the Sankhya Dance Company.

Three students from Vaibhav Arekar's Sankhya Dance Company, Gautam Marathe, Poorva Saraswat and Eesha Pinglay, performed for the Nritya Gurukul initiative. The three dancers reflected Vaibhav sir’s work, especially his emphasis on body conditioning, as it was quite visible through their structured and very disciplined Nritta movements that they showed throughout the entire performance. The program began with a Mallari in Raagam Nattai and Mishra Trikuta Taalam. It was a very powerful start and instantly anyone who knew Vaibhav sir could recognize that Gautam, Poorva and Eesha were his students. Although under their Guru, they all brought their own unique identity and distinctive perspective to the performance with the Mallari, which is largely a Nritta-based piece. The three dancers were able to capture the entire stage with their movements and allowed for formations that were quite unique.

Then the three dancers performed solos, giving each performer a chance to shine. The first solo was performed by Gautam Marathe. He performed the very famous padam Yaro Ivar Yaro in Adi Taalam in Raagam Bhairavi. This padam talks about the exact moment when Lord Rama, who entered Mithila and saw Devi Sita for the very first time high up in the palace tower. He gazed at her, not knowing that love had blossomed in his heart. Gautam was able to portray both the roles of a curious and love-struck Ram, while also a conscious and shy Sita, who was up in the palace tower gazing at Lord Ram below. His abhinaya was soft, expressive and it was very amusing and very beautiful to see a male dancer perform a padam like this. I have not seen that many male Bharatanatyam dancers perform and it was very eye-opening and a wonderful experience to see and witness a male Bharatanatyam dancer perform.

Following that was another padam performed by Poorva Saraswat, Ennatavam Seydanai Yasoda, in Taalam Adi and Raagam Kapi. In this padam, the poet questions Yashoda as to how and what she did to get Lord Krishna as her baby? What kind of penance did she do? Did she do any kind of magic? The poet poses all these questions to Yashoda in a very fun, amusing and curious manner. Poorva, who recently had become a mother, was able to connect it with her real-life experience. Poorva's abhinaya was quite realistic, giving that motherly care and showing the same adoration and love that Yashoda would have had for her baby Krishna. This padam is an all-time classic and favorite and every time I see it performed, it just warms my heart and I'm pretty sure it did the same with audiences as well.

The last solo was performed by Eesha Pinglay. Eesha performed a wonderful abhanga called Virahini Ghani Vaje Guna Guna in Raagam Mishra and Mishra Chapu Taalam. In this abhanga, the poet Sant Dnyaneshwar talks about how his body and the surrounding changes, all because of Lord Vitthala. Eesha was able to bring in a beautiful aspect of very slow and expressive abhinaya and she was able to create the atmosphere of peace, solitude and this mere effect that Lord Vitthala had on Sant Dnyaneshwar. One thing I noticed was all solos were padams, which allowed the abhinaya of the performers to shine more and the first composition performed, that is the Mallari, allowed for the Nritta to shine.

And the last composition, which was a group performance by all three, which concluded and showed both Nritta and Natya of dance, was a fantastic Kali performance in Raaga Malika and Taal Malika. The dancers were quite in fierce form as they showed the different iconographies of Goddess Kali in her fierce, terrifying poses. This composition and the performance showed the abhinaya in a very interesting manner, as being soft as well as fierce and there was equal focus on the nritta, which gave a balanced structure and architecture to the entire composition.

The five performances performed by Vaibhav Arekar's students Gautam Marathe, Poorva Saraswat and Eesha Pinglay brought in a standing ovation as the performance came to an end. The Nritya Gurukul initiative helped the three students of Vaibhav Arekar to gain a lot of experience about performance management. It was very wonderful to see this performance and to expand my horizons to different dancers, different performers and I'm looking forward to attending more programs from Vaibhav Arekar’s Sankhya Dance Company.
