Vakrākāra: An ode to their Guru and to new beginnings by Anoushka, Daquil and Namaha

When it comes to the very aspect of the Guru-shishya parampara in the classical arts world, or rather the artistic world today, there is one significant aspect which existed centuries ago, which was of the Gurukula-style tradition. The students would live with their Gurus in their houses and learn from them every day until they were ready to set out in the real world with all that they had learned from their Gurus. But this was not just applicable to normal everyday education. This was applicable to all aspects of life, whether it was music, whether it was dance, whether it was social values and much more. Today, with the rise of modernization, there is a depletion in this Gurukula-style tradition. However, there is one institution that stands tall as a reminder about the Gurukula tradition, about the importance of the Guru-shishya parampara and how important it is to devote oneself to the art form completely.

It was Nrityagram that was invited for the NCPA's Nritya Gurukul tradition initiative, which was supported by Bank of America, wherein students from different Gurus all over India had gotten a chance from NCPA, to learn different aspects about performance management, as how important it is to sustain in the artistic world today. This training helped them understand how to set up a performance, how to read a contract and understand the legalities as well as the financial terminologies of a contract, how to understand different aspects of lighting, sound design, costuming, makeup and much more. Nritya Gurukul had lots of Gurus perform and Nrityagram stood as one of the finest dance schools to have presented three students under Guru Surupa Sen.

Nrityagram was founded by Protima Gauri in 1990 and it literally means ‘Dance Village’. It is a unique artistic community near the outskirts of Bengaluru, where classical dance becomes the very life. Protima Gauri was inspired by the traditional Guru-shishya parampara and wanted a space where dancers could completely devote themselves in the art form and over the years this space has evolved into an internationally recognized center for classical dance, particularly Odissi, while continuing to nurture education, performance and artistic experimentation.

And today it has been taken to newer heights under the leadership and the creative direction of Guru Surupa Sen, who arrived at Nrityagram intending to study Odissi for just a few months, but she committed herself to the institution and became its first graduating student. Surupa Sen has studied under masters Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra and Protima Gauri and is now the artistic director of one of contemporary Odissi's most distinctive choreographical institutions. Surupa Sen's work respects the foundation of classical dance while constantly questioning new possibilities in movement, rhythm, space and storytelling. She has showcased numerous successful and famous productions, which demonstrate her skills to engage with contemporary ideas without abandoning tradition. Through her works, through her performances, along with the Nrityagram dance ensemble, the institution has carried the distinctive interpretation laid down by Protima Gauri to audiences across India and the world. And this was quite evident through the performance of Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala and Namaha Mazoomdar, who performed for the Nritya Gurukul initiative.

The presentation of the evening was titled Vakrākāra, with four compositions set to perform by the three dancers, Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala and Namaha Mazoomdar. The performance for the evening began with a wonderful, hopeful presentation called Abha, Invocation to Light, where the three dancers walked onto stage with oil lamps, indicating the emergence of lightness, hope, within the midst of darkness and uncertainty. The composition was a self-choreography by Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala and Namaha Mazoomdar mentored by their Guru. The self choreographed was quite well executed and it was such that it showed the wonderful techniques of Guru Surupa Sen's artistic abilities, while the three dancers were able to bring their own uniqueness within their movements, which gave such an interesting flair to the performance.

Following Abha was Ritu Vasant, which was an abstract dance performed by Anoushka Rahman as a solo. Surupa Sen's choreography is always known for her complex footwork, complex combination of movements, while also pushing forward the aesthetic of Odissi, with a strong emphasis on unique formations. Ritu Vasant talked about the season of spring. This wonderful performance by Anoushka was a beautiful display of her interpretation through Surupa Sen's choreography of the wonderful Vasant Ritu. Through abstract movements, Anoushka was able to create how the nature and environment turns into a bliss of spring when the season of spring arrives, with wonderful birds chirping, the nature swaying with trees turning lush green.

After this wonderful Ritu Vasant performance was Muroli Paani, which is Abhinaya, set to an Oriya song, performed by Anoushka Rahman, Daquil Miriyala and Namaha Mazoomdar. Muroli Paani was a wonderful display of traditional and quintessential Surupa Sen's choreography. The sound and the composition was set in such a melodious manner that it beautifully complemented the Odissi movements performed by the three dancers. Muroli Paani stands as my favorite performance from the evening. It was a refreshing composition with wonderful choreography and the three dancers were able to demonstrate their talent effortlessly in this composition.

After Muroli Paani was Shiva Ashtakam, performed by Anoushka Rahman and Daquil Miriyala as an ode to Shiva, as well as the ferocious form of Batuk Bhairava. The two dancers were able to show the fierce forms of Lord Shiva through their Abhinaya, which was quite expressive, while also showing fiery and powerful movements, which Odissi demands. As Surupa Sen's choreography is always significant because of her unique formations, this was quite visible through this performance as well as through Muroli Paani as well. The two dancers played along with the stage, capturing the stage. Although just two, they were able to fill out the stage with their wonderful movements.

The music composition was done by Pandit Raghunath Pranigrahi for the first three compositions and for Abha it was done by Jatin Sahu. With the wonderful recorded music with musicians Rajendra Swain on lead vocal & harmonium, Buddhanath Swain on chants & mardala, Srinibas Satapathy on bamboo flute, Sanjib Kunda on violin and Surupa Sen manjira. While for Aabha it was Jatin Sahu on lead vocal & harmonium, Rohan Dahale on chants, mardala & manjira, Vijay Tambe on bamboo flute and Aparna Deodhar sitar, this performance concluded with a standing ovation for the wonderful students of Surupa Sen, as well as for their Guru as well. The three students, Anoushka, Daquil and Namaha, were able to pay obeisance to their Guru while also showing the audience how far they have come and how much they deserve more chances to perform as wonderful performers that they are today.
