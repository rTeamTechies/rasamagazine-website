The Doyenne of Abhinaya.

Priyadarshini Govind doesn’t just do abhinaya, she creates magic.

Photo credits - The Hindu

Out of the eight Indian classical dances is Bharatanatyam. It is the oldest and the first classical dance to have ever emerged. It has been a classical dance tradition that has been shaped over centuries through the contributions of devoted dancers and gurus who have done their work of preserving this tradition while also reinterpreting its possibilities. Among the most notable and foremost dancers is the exponent of contemporary India, the iconic Bharatanatyam dancer, Priyadarshini Govind. She is a dancer, choreographer, and a teacher who is widely known for her depth and understanding of abhinaya, and redefining techniques. She has contributed magnanimously to Bharatanatyam, not only as a performer, but also as a teacher and a cultural administrator.

Priyadarshini Govind was born in Chennai, which is a city that remains a central hub for Indian classical arts. She began her training in Bharatanatyam from a very young age and had her Arangetram at the age of nine, starting the journey as a dancer. Her initial training happened under Guru K. Rajaratnam, who trained her in the Vazhuvoor style of Bharatanatyam. This phase of her life helped her lay down a strong foundation, helping her familiarise with the essential vocabulary of the dance, including adavus, rhythm, posture, and different elements of the Vazhuvoor bani.

“My Guru Smt. Kalanidhi Narayanan had two distinct personalities - one where she lived her life as a very traditional Brahmin woman following her principles and the other as a teacher, she was the most liberal person”.

A major turning point in her artistic training life came across when she started training under her Guru Kalanidhi Narayanan. Guru Kalanidhi Narayanan was one of the most known and venerated figures in Bharatanatyam, who was especially known for her mastery over abhinaya. Under her, Priyadarshini Govind went across intensive training in expressive abhinaya, dance, learning to understand and interpret classical poetry with a lot of emotional depth, subtlety, as well as restraint. This phase of her life helped her transform her artistic identity and establish her as the leading dancer and interpreter of abhinaya in contemporary Bharatanatyam.

A turning point in her artistic career happened in 2013, as she was appointed as the director of Kalakshetra Foundation. Founded by Rukmini Devi Arundale in 1936, Kalakshetra is India’s most prestigious institution for the study and promotion of classical arts, especially Bharatanatyam. Kalakshetra is known for its distinctive style and emphasis on discipline and aesthetic beauty. Priyadarshini Govind’s appointment as the director was huge and a significant step as she was the first director, who was not a student of Kalakshetra institution.

“To me at that time, Kalakshetra represented a technique of doing Adavus in a particular way, represented a technique of presenting dance productions and there were certain compositions that were unique to Kalakshetra”.

Her leadership reflected an intersection of different artistic lineages within Bharatanatyam. During her tenure from 2013 to 2017, she contributed to both the administrative as well as the artistic functioning of the institution. She was highly involved in a lot of academic programs and enhancing outreach initiatives and engaging with a lot of younger and broader audiences. She strived to make the classical arts more accessible for young kids which included conducting programs for schools and young learners. She has put efforts and has participated in multiple initiatives which aimed at preserving the heritage of Kalakshetra, such as the documentation of costumes, traditions, and production practices. Her directorship was one which reflected the respect for the institution's legacy, but also addressed contemporary challenges in arts education.

The main balance of Priyadarshini Govind’s artistry lies in her ability to blend Nritta which is pure dance and Abhinaya in a seamless manner. She is particularly acclaimed for her performances of different padams and javalis, with her execution reflecting her discipline and clarity. One of her most well known interpreted pieces is Krishna Ne Begane Baaro, which is a Kannada devotional composition, traditionally attributed to Vyasatirtha. Her interpretation and understanding of the piece brings out the maternal devotion of Yashoda as she calls out to her child. Her performance in this particular piece is marked by natural playfulness, tenderness, and emotional intimacy, capturing both the dignity and the human charm of Krishna and Yashoda. One of her most famous works is that of Ravan who was the multifaceted antagonist of the Ramayana. Her portrayal of Ravan in the composition shows how nuanced her abhinaya is and how it can be differently interpreted in different contexts. Through her expressive vocabulary, she presents Ravan as a tragic and complex character, which highlights the moral and emotional ambiguities within the classical narrative.

Beyond performance and choreography, Priyadarshini Govind has made significant contributions as an educator and a facilitator. She has trained multiple students and conducted workshops and lecture demonstrations, both in India as well as on international stages. Her technique of teaching emphasises on understanding the literary music and emotional dimensions of Bharatanatyam and she encourages to engage with meaning behind the moments, while fostering a deeper appreciation of the art form. She has performed extensively across India and internationally, representing the artform on major stages around the world. She has presented her choreographies across different festivals and venues in India, the United States, Singapore and Europe. In 2022, she collaborated with director Shruti Harihara Subramanyam on the dance film Yavanika, which was a media content which explored themes of self and illusion, which was adapted for Digital audiences during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Her contributions to the art world have been recognised through several prestigious accolades. She has received the Kalaimamani award from the Government of Tamil Nadu. The Sangeet Natak Akademi award, which is one of the highest honours in the performing arts world as well as titles like, Nritya Choodamani from Krishna Gana Sabha. What distinguishes Priyadarshini Govind in the contemporary landscape is her emphasis and her understanding of Abhinaya as a core of Bharatanatyam. In an era where visual spectacle can sometimes overshadow the understanding of emotions, her repertoire serves as a reminder of the art form’s narrative and expressive essence. Her career reflects the interconnected roles of a performer, teacher and administrator in sustaining the classical arts through her teaching, she has always ensured the transmission of knowledge and through her institutional work she has contributed to the structure and support of the art.

She stands today as a significant figure in the world of Bharatanatyam through her work. She has not only done the job of preserving a classical tradition, but also reaffirming its enduring power to communicate, inspire and connect across time and culture.
