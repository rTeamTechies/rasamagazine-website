The invincible Maestro of the Saraswati Veena.

Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh doesn’t just play the Veena, she invokes Goddess Saraswati herself!

When it comes to Indian classical music and Indian classical music instruments, a lot of the time people think about the Sitar or the Tanpura, the Tabla or the Mrudangam, but very few people talk about the Saraswati Veena. And one person took it upon herself to make it her life’s goal to bring attention of the world to the beauty of the Saraswati Veena and that person is none other than Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh. She stands out today as one of the most celebrated exponents of the Saraswati Veena. Through her decades of practice, performances, passion, as well as dedication, she has completely changed how people view the Veena as a traditional classical instrument into an instrument which is globally appreciated for its musical voice. Dr Jayanthi Karesh is the most influential person in Carnatic music today because of her expressive playing style, pedagogy, and innovative concert format.

“I want to share the voice of this instrument called Saraswati Veena to the world, it in a way represents the voice of India for me”

She was born in 1969 in Bengaluru into a very distinguished family of Carnatic musicians. She is the sixth generation musician in the family with her maternal uncle, the legendary Violin Maestro Lalgudi. G.Jayaraman.

“I come from a family of musicians, and they have been musicians for the last six generations so it wasn’t surprising that I took music as my profession and passion”.

Music was not just an art form in her household. It was a way of life. Her introduction to music at a young age. Her mother Lalgudi Rajalakshmi was herself a musician, and in a family of violinists, she wanted her daughter to start off with the Veena. According to Jayanthi Kumaresh, it was as if the instrument called out to her, and she started playing with the Veena at the age of three.

Photo credits - Jayanthi Kumaresh Website

Growing up, especially in a family that is so musically enriched meant that practice and musical discipline was a part of her daily routine. Her childhood passed by listening to Carnatic musicians and attending concerts as well as learning lessons in musical nuances from senior members of her family. Unlike many children today who are pushed into classical music training, reluctantly, Jayanthi had developed a genuine affection for the Veena at a very early stage in life.

Her formal training began under her mother, Lalgudi Rajalakshmi, and later it continued under her aunt Smt. Padmavathy Ananthagopalan, who herself was a very renowned and respected Veena artist. Her aunt at the time was living in Singapore, teaching the Veena to multiple students. It was only when she moved back to India that Jayanthi made a request to her mother to learn from her aunt in Chennai. Her mother thought that Jayanthi would return in another two days, but she stayed there for almost 22 to 24 years, completing her education, her bachelors in English Literature, Masters in English Literature and much more.

“My mother’s elder sister and my Guru, Smt. Padmavathi Anant Gopalan, was the first person I saw who played the Veena in a family full of violinists”.

Later on, she trained under the legendary Veena Maestro Dr.S.Balachander, who is known for his unconventional and sophisticated style which has deeply influenced her playing techniques.

Apart from these direct gurus, she has also received knowledge, inspiration and guidance from musicians like T.Brinda, Thanjavur Sankara Iyer and T.R .Subrahmanyam, however, her uncle Lalgudi Jayaraman has remained one of her strongest musical influences throughout her life.

There are multiple musical instruments that are there in Carnatic music and any instrument that can be played with a string is called Veena and the Saraswati Veena is one of the oldest and most sacred instruments in the Indian classical music tradition. It is usually associated with Goddess Saraswati, who is the Goddess of knowledge and arts. And due to this reason, the Veena carries immense cultural and spiritual significance, but by the late 20th century, the Saraswati Veena was facing challenges in terms of popularity and accessibility among the youth and younger audiences.

This was a time when Jayanthi Kumaresh made a very important decision. When other musicians were choosing more commercially visible instruments or directly vocal music, she chose to devote herself entirely to Saraswati Veena. Her commitment to this divine instrument was not merely just technical, it had a lot of emotional and philosophical connection. For her, the Veena is an extension of the human voice and sought to make it sing through her plane. This approach of Jayanthi Kumaresh helped her develop a very highly lyrical style known for its Gayaki quality, which is a style that imitates vocal music.

Over the period of time, she has evolved her own unique style by combining traditional Carnatic traditions with innovative tone techniques and expressive tuning. She uses this distinctive approach to not only appeal to the classical audiences, but also younger listeners who were quite unfamiliar with Carnatic music.

Apart from being such an accomplished artist, Dr Jayanthi Kumaresh is also an accomplished scholar. She has completed her postgraduate studies in English literature and later on pursued a doctoral research in music at the University of Mysore. Her thesis focused on an analytical study of various banis or stylistic traditions and playing techniques of the Saraswati Veena.

Her deep commitment to the preservation and the understanding of the technical and historical richness of the Veena tradition is reflected by her immense academic work. She has presented multiple scholarly papers at multiple conferences and has conducted lecture demonstrations at institutions such as the Madras Music Academy. She has explored multiple topics, including styles of Veena, raga development, and the role of Thaanam in Carnatic music with such immense passion and devotion to not only artistry, but also scholarship, she has become one of the most respected intellectual voices in Carnatic instrumental music tradition.

Dr Jayanti Kumar’s concert career is more than four decades. She has performed extensively across India and internationally representing the divine Saraswati Veena and multiple prestigious global stages. Her performances are known and celebrated for their technical, brilliant, emotional depth and meditative quality. She has toured the entire world and has performed at global venues, which are renowned and festivals across the world, such as appearances in Europe, the United States, The Middle East and Australia. She has also performed at the Darbar festival in London, the Sydney Opera House and many more.

Her concerts often go beyond the traditional Carnatic concert formats. She is rooted in classical grammar, but she experiments with presentation styles. One of her biggest strengths lies in making classical music accessible to the common public without losing its true essence. She has collaborated with musicians from different traditions, including the late Tabla Maestro Ustad, Zakir Hussain. Such collaborations of her, demonstrate the adaptability of the Veena and she has introduced Carnatic music to a wider audience.

In addition to her solo concert, she has also made fusion and world music projects through her ensemble called ‘Indian Spice’ and through these projects, she has explored the connection between Indian classical music and global music traditions.

Dr. Jayanti Kumaresh has always been quite innovative when it comes to her concert and thematic productions. She has that innate ability to maintain the respect for tradition, but also try something new, which will keep everyone on their toes. Amongst these is the ‘45 Ragas’ concert, where she performed a continuous performance of ragas without kritis or percussion accompaniment, another innovative production of her was the ‘Colours of Kalyani’ concert entirely dedicated to the Raga Kalyani. She also created another innovative concert format called ‘Story in Concert’ where there is a combination of narration, painting and music to create a multisensory artistic experience. Such productions of her show her vision of music as not just music, but also a capable medium of storytelling, emotional communication and cultural education. She has also composed and contributed music to dance productions and orchestral works, including Krishna Bhakti, Abbakarani and Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya. She has also composed for the National South Indian Youth Orchestra in the United Kingdom.

A very interesting initiative by her is a widely known popular web series, which launched in 2016 called the Cup O’ Carnatic show. It was a series designed to simplify and popularise Carnatic music for young contemporary audiences and beginners.

The main idea of the series was simple but quite revolutionary. Dr. Jayanthi is in was to explain Carnatic concepts in short, engaging easy to understand episodes. This is a time before reels even existed through the series. She introduced audiences to ragas, compositions, tala systems, and musical aesthetics in a very friendly and accessible manner.

Another important aim was to remove the misconception that Carnatic music is too complicated or inaccessible for ordinary listeners. She used relatable examples such as playing the iPhone caller tune on the Veena and many more methods as well as visual presentations to bridge the gap between traditional classical music and modern digital audiences.

The series became quite popular and has accumulated millions of views, which has also inspired many young people to take an interest in the field of Carnatic music and especially the Veena.

After the success of the digital series, Dr Jayanthi Kumaresh created a concert based on the series. The Cup O’ Carnatic life stage experience. She has turned it into a live show which combines music animation, storytelling and audience interaction and she has performed in cities like Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai and has received tremendous praise. A personal note that I’d like to tell is that I was fortunate enough to attend a Cup O’ Carnatic show when it happened at NMACC in Mumbai last year. I was there, not knowing anything about what exactly was Cup O’ Carnatic, I did not know about the digital series, nor did I know about the live event, but one thing for sure is that I left with a lot of memories, knowledge and and immense respect, praise and admiration for Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh. This entire show was not just a performance, but it was a magical way for her to explain to the young audiences as well as people who do not know anything about Carnatic music, what the beauty that is, Carnatic music.

Throughout her prestigious career, Dr. Jayanti Kumaresh has achieved and received numerous prestigious awards, honours and accolades for her contribution to Indian classical music. In 2022, she received the very prestigious Sangeet Natak Akademi award from the Government of India, which is among the highest national honours given to performing artists in the country. She has also received the Kalaimamani award from the government of Tamil Nadu, which is an honour which is given to distinguished artists in the state. She has been given the Indira Shivam endowment medal, The Sangeet Shikhar Samman from Bharathiya Vidya Bhavan and The KS Narayan Swami award for Veena. In 2026, she was selected for the highly known, prestigious and respected Sangeeta Kalanidhi award by the Madras Music Academy. This award is known as one of the greatest honours in Carnatic music. Her selection for this award is particularly significant because it has been after several decades that a Veena artist has received this award.

Today, Dr. Jayanti Kumaresh has contributed beyond performance. She is also a culture ambassador for not just the Saraswathi Veena, but also for Indian classical music and she plays a major role in the preservation as well as modernisation of Carnatic instrument music.

Through her concerts, her research as well as doing online education and conducting master classes she has introduced and opened up the avenue of the beauty of the Veena to countless listeners. She has also actively engaged in teaching, tutoring and mentoring through the Jayanthi Kumaresh Academy for Veena, which was founded in 2021, which is an academy, which offers online learning opportunities for students around the world to learn and explore the beauty of the Veena.

Her efforts have definitely paid off and created a movement in reviving interest in the young generation as well as visibility for Indian classical music and Saraswati Meena

In many ways, it can be considered that Dr. Jayanthi Kumaresh represents the perfect balance between one side of tradition and the other side of innovation. Although she remains deeply rooted in the classical Carnatic traditions, she’s also exploring new methods of communication, education as well as artistic presentation. Through various outreach programmes, through her music, through her scholarship, she has made sure that the voice of the Saraswati Veena continues to resonate powerfully in the modern world and will never lose its beauty.
