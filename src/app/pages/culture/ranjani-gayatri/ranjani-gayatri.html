Ranjani-Gayatri: The sisters who changed the world of Carnatic Music for the new era

On the 23rd of the month of June 2026, the Carnatic vocalist sister duo, Ranjani, and Gayatri were awarded with the Padma Shri award. The announcement of their awards had created an already widespread appreciation from different and celebrated musicians, scholars and rasikas all around the world. For almost four decades, the sisters have created a unique place in the world of Indian classical music where they are doing their duty of bridging tradition along with innovation, scholarship, emotion, technical mastery and accessibility. Today, the Padma Shri award is not just recognition for their excellence as performance, but it also serves as an acknowledgement of their never-ending contribution to the preservation and the expansion of Carnatic music in this 21st century.

Ranjini and Gayatri are one of the most celebrated singers today and are considered synonymous to musical excellence today. The sister duo have graced the world by performing at packed auditoriums in cities like Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, London, New York, Singapore, Sydney, and many more.

Ranjani and Gayatri’s father, N. Balasubramanian, was a passionate listener and a connoisseur of classical music while their mother, Meenakshi Balasubramanian, was a trained Carnatic vocalist, so it was very common for the two sisters to have the influence of music, culture, and devotion already present in their house. Gayatri at merely the age of two and half years old was able to identify ragas while Ranjini displayed remarkable rhythmic awareness from a very early age. The two sisters are naturally gifted and were nurtured carefully by their parents to make sure that they grew their love for Carnatic music.

But before they established themselves as singers, Ranjini and Gayatri were first established violinists. They began learning the violin under Sangeeta Bhushanam Professor. T. S. Krishnaswami at the Shanmukhananda Sangeet Vidyalaya in Mumbai. It was under his guidance that they were able to create and develop a very deep understanding of melody, gamakas, phrasing, and the structure of different ragas. Because of the discipline for the violin, it helped them sharpen their listening skills and it helped furthermore to create an analytical understanding of Carnatic music.

Their initial vocal training came from their mother who led the foundation for their career and their love for Carnatic music. But it was the legendary Carnatic musician Padma Bhushan Sangita Kalanidhi P. S. Narayanaswamy who completely transformed the sisters’ artistic journey when they were learning under him. Narayanaswamy is known as the finest gurus in Carnatic music because of his unwavering and uncompromising emphasis on lyrical purity and sticking to tradition.

He profoundly influenced Ranjani and Gayatri’s approach to music because of his teachings. It helped them understand the importance of Sahitya, which is lyrics, the Bhava, which are the emotions and Manodharma, which means creative improvisation. And it was under his guidance that they were able to go from successful violinists to exceptional calibre vocalists. Their vocal career formally began in the late 1990s, and it was guaranteed that it was going to go on a rise from there itself.

They performed for a very long time as violinists with several stalwarts of Carnatic music, and this was the experience that helped them to gain insight in concert planning, improvisation, how to build one’s repertoire and stage crafting. And one can notice this background in their vocal performances today because when they perform the elaborations within a particular Raga, one can notice the precision and fluidity that is always associated with the violin. They have a strong command over melodic structures which allows them to navigate and create improvisational passages with proper clarity and confidence.

Today they are firmly rooted in tradition, but at the same time, they experiment and expand the possibilities of the concert format, which is why it makes them stand out in the forum of Carnatic music.

The concerts are always grounded in the traditional Carnatic compositions and grammar with the respect for compositions of Thyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri, Papanasam Sivan, Purandaradasa, Annamacharya, and several other composers. They are able to bring out the exact emotions that the composers have put forward in that composition which is a very difficult thing to do and they able to carefully explore the Raga in a concert layer by layer rather than jumping directly into it with speed or technical display.

What is the most important characteristic of their music today is the coordination and complimentary activity that happens as a duo. The sisters possess an almost telepathic musical connection, which allows them to navigate intricate, melodic and rhythmic passages without even having to have a conversation, which is what makes this duo very special.

Ranjani and Gayatri are known, especially for their Raagam Thanam Pallavi, which is often considered as the pinnacle of Carnatic improvisation. I personally have listened to their performance more than once, and I can say with full confidence that I have not heard a Raagam Thanam Pallavi as great as theirs. They have such control over the notes of ragas that they are performing in the Raagam Thanam Pallavi that it almost looks artificial, like robots because how can something like this be so human. It feels too perfect and that is the product of their hard work, their efforts and their training as well as their love for Carnatic music.

It is the expensive repertoire of Ranjani and Gayatri that also is quite impressive because they not only perform compositions in Sanskrit or Tamil, but they also perform in Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, and several other songs. They bring compositions from different regions and different traditions and explore them in such a beautiful musical way that it leaves the audience in awe. Another aspect of their repertoire is the presentation of abhangs, which are devotional songs associated with the Bhakti traditions in Maharashtra, and as someone who has heard these abhangs in person, I can say that Ranjani and Gayatri do absolute justice to the renditions of abhangs. They are considered as an audience favourite across linguistic and regional boundaries.

Today Ranjani and Gayatri, have not only emerged as great performers, but they have also been actively working as composers, educators and ambassadors of Indian classical music. They have composed and tuned several devotional works like abhangs and bhajans, which reflect their sensitivity to both lyrical meaning and melodic aesthetics.

They have conducted several workshops, lecture demonstrations, and master classes across India and abroad. They have a particular teaching style which emphasises on the deeper, philosophical and aesthetic meaning rather than completely relying on technical abilities. They have actively been working as ambassadors to bring global awareness to Carnatic music, especially among the younger generations and diaspora communities all around.

They have performed extensively across North America, Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Southeast Asia, and the Middle East and they are constantly touring regularly with performing at prestigious music festivals, cultural institutions, bringing together audiences who love Carnatic music and the Sister duo. Their performances are such that it introduces thousands of listeners to the richness of South Indian classical music which is very important today and it is very important to acknowledge that international success because Carnatic music is such that it requires a considerable amount of attention, engagement from listeners, but the sisters have proved that there are enthusiastic audiences across cultures language to attend and appreciate Carnatic music.

They have been honoured with the Kalaimamani award from the Government of Tamil Nadu, the Sanskriti Puraskar, the National Eminence Award, the Kalki Krishnamurthy award and numerous titles which have been conferred by leading Sabhas and cultural institutions all over the country. They have also received the Indira Sivasailam Endowment Medal as well as several prestigious recognitions within the Carnatic community.

And because of the contributions that Ranjani and Gayatri have put in to take Carnatic music to a global level, they have been recognised by the government of India with the Padma Shri. As someone who has been an admirer of Ranjini and Gayatri for their talent, their skills and their love for Carnatic music, it feels correct to see them get the recognition that they deserve.

Their journey from child prodigy to acclaimed vocalist reflects decades of dedication to the art form. They are able to assess Carnatic music through meanings, aesthetic, philosophical backgrounds, as well as the technical brilliance of Carnatic music. They have always maintained relevance with the rapidly changing world to make sure that audiences are always connected to this wonderful form of Indian classical music even today and they are celebrated for it.

In the history of modern Carnatic music, the names Ranjani and Gayatri have undoubtedly taken up a special place, but not just as celebrated performers, but also as artists who have helped shape the sound, the spirit of Carnatic music in the new generation.
