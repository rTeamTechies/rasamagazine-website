Mumbai…still the city of dreams

By Shree Rawandale

Photo credits - Citizen Matters

I remember growing up, I was always told that Mumbai is the city of dreams, cinematic and gorgeous, it felt like something more than just a city; it felt like a promise, the kind that arrives wrapped in stories. With a distant relative’s arrival, in grandma’s voice, right before you fall asleep. It never promised to be “easy” but it was supposed to be worth it. But nowadays, it feels like the city is burning, with basic necessities like clean air and clean water being treated like luxuries. People wake up checking the AQI like a fever reading. Water filters, air purifiers have become more common than ceiling fans. Parents have to worry about the air their children are breathing more than the school they go to. It’s sad, the fact that in order to “make it” you no longer just need to shift to Mumbai. It now includes a hidden checklist: ‘Can I afford an apartment with reliable water?’ ‘Can I work from home on bad air days?’ ‘Can my child digest unclean water?’ Mumbai meant success. But that “success” now suddenly has a criteria, a cutoff. You need a certain amount of riches, a certain amount of health, in order to “make it” or perhaps in order to just stay alive.

And yet, the city keeps running. Local trains still start at 9 AM. Chai stalls still do crazy business. The India Gate still sees millions of tourists daily. Ferries take families to their villages. People still move here. It is nothing other than desperation stirring beneath an unbroken surface, outward calm masking an inner turmoil. This calm isn’t genuine, it’s a delusion the city feeds to its people. The city of dreams hasn’t died, it’s just metamorphosed. It now requires a bargain. You must sacrifice the wellness of your lungs, your peace of mind, a peaceful night’s sleep, all in exchange for success that is often sub-par. It now feels more like a transaction, an unfair exchange, where the city takes everything, returns the bare minimum and calls that generosity. And still, we stay. For a dream sold to us when we were mere children, or perhaps for the unjustifiable hope, that someday, Mumbai will return to its prime, to the glory we were once narrated with such pride. This is the lullaby, we hum to ourselves while laying face down in an apartment we often cannot afford. Tell me then, is it really still the city of dreams?
