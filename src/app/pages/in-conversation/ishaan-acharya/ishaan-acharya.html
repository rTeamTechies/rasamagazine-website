In Conversation

‘Do what you love, love what you do’ — Dr. Ishaan Acharya’s mantra in life!

In a sit down interview, Dr. Ishaan recounts his start into animal rescue and the joys of his journey.

When you talk about the history of this very earth, you cannot leave something that is so important to the world to this planet and everyone, that is nature. We are all a part of nature and nature is a part of us, but beyond humans, there are other beings that exist and the first ones to roam this earth before we even evolved into Homosapiens. These are precious animals. They are the ones who in a way created and are maintaining the cycle of life and death. They are the ones who maintain the balance between the population of nature, and nature respects them. But these animals cannot speak at least in human languages. So how do we help them? How do we treat them? How do we make their life better and that’s when the role of an animal doctor, an animal rescuer comes in. And I was able to interview an animal doctor and a wildlife rescuer who happens to be my school senior and he is someone who has achieved so much at such a young age of 23. That is Dr. Ishaan Acharya.

Ishaan’s passion for animals began at a very young age when he visited Phaltan, his mother’s native place where he used to hang amongst around all the domestic animals that he had seen in villages with his grandfather. His love for animals somewhat started in that area and it started to grow after he went to the Kanha National Park for a trek at the age of five years old. At this Park, he saw his first tiger. He does not remember the sighting, but that was the start of his true passion for animals.

“Being a vet is not a standard profession that everyone goes for so a lot of people ask me this question and I have a photo of me when I was two years old with a stethoscope and like small animals and I was checking them.”

This itself is a testament to his very young passion for animals and for their betterment. Ishaan admits that he does get a lot of mixed reactions from people when he discloses that he is an animal doctor. People from the young generation are very curious and excited to know what exactly is an animal doctor's job like, but there are some boomers or the older generation who have taunted him, but he did not pay any attention to any of those comments.

Becoming a vet is something Ishaan wanted to achieve, and he’s enjoying it to the fullest. His trajectory was impacted by majorly by Jim Corbett, who was a hunter who turned into conservationist, but Ishaan would rather focus on the conservationist part, but along with Jim Corbett David Attenborough Jane Goodall were amongst the other few conservationists and environmentalists who have completely changed his perspective on animal rescue.

In India, becoming an animal doctor is quite similar to the process of becoming a normal doctor. You have to give the very tough Neet exam and do a 5 and a half year course of the MBBS program, but the difference is the subjects. In the last years of the course the students get to actually practice as intern doctors with animals that come in for treatment. Ishaan studied at the very prestigious Mumbai Veterinary Hospital located in Parel and for him, the experience was quite thrilling because he got to work with a lot of animals, but his passion lies for exotic animals like reptiles and much more.

To common people the task and the concept of animal rescue and an animal doctor might sound very interesting, but in actuality, it can be very emotionally intense for the doctor. This goes similarly for human doctors as well, especially in times of emergencies, surgeries, and extreme terrifying situations where the patient is in critical health, but in the case of the humans, the patients can communicate at times what they are feeling, the distress or the pain that they are going through, but in this case of animals they cannot. Ishaan thinks that this side of being an animal doctor and handling an animal who is in extreme critical health can have a lot of mental stress on the doctor. And he also agrees that this is a very not talked about aspect when it comes to being an animal doctor, especially here in India because it can lead to a lot of depression. For Ishaan, it is very important that he separates the emotional side and the duty side when it comes to functioning in surgeries or treating a critical animal, which is very hard to do today because sentiments can get caught up with duty and not in a doctor's life in any profession.

Being an animal doctor always needs to come with a prerequisite according to Ishaan, which is very important, which is having compassion for animals.

“I would say that. You also need a passion for it because this is not an easy profession.”

Which is very true, and I think this should be taken seriously for all the jobs because today, yes, living and earning money can be quite hard, but if you’re not enjoying it, are you truly living, is the question?

“Do what you love, love what you do”

Ishaan’s mantra, after Mumbai Veterinary College

“Do what you love, love what you do”, is Ishaan‘s takeaway and a sort of mantra from his entire education process journey after graduating from the Mumbai Veterinary College. Ishaan is in a place because of his correct choice to pursue a passion filled job. Being an animal doctor has always been a dream for him, and for him to pursue an education, and then become an animal doctor, for him he is the happiest. He also admits that he has friends who are doing very cliche typical jobs of lawyers, engineers which are equally important as well, but they are not happy according to him. So Ishaan feels quite fortunate and lucky enough to be in a profession that he loves and is quite happy doing it.

Ishaan’s professional work life stems from his internships and has gone beyond borders. He has worked with numerous organisations here in Maharashtra itself and for him, it was very important to get that on ground experience and for that he was ready to work with any organisation that took him. Wildlife internships are limited because they are regulated by the Forest Department, but there are a lot of job opportunities available in the field of an animal doctor here in metropolitan cities like Mumbai, so he has interned at multiple clinics under numerous doctors to gain hands-on experience on how to deal with domestic animals.

With domestic animals and wild animals which are two different things, wild animals need to be treated with the least amount of stress and the least amount of human imprinting done on them. According to Ishaan, it is very important that these wild animals which are treated and then left back into the forest should be still afraid of humans that they do not come back to get food from the humans that have treated them. While domestic animals are quite easy to treat since they are smaller in size, and they love and are familiar with the human environment, unlike wild animals.

According to Ishaan, veterinary is a field which is quite unsaturated, in terms of job. There are lots of jobs available at least in Mumbai and he says that it’s a decent paying job with long hours, but you have a confirmed job for sure.

I think every animal rescuer and animal doctor has certain cases that they have dealt with which really stick with them and turn into a memory. Ishaan has worked with such interesting cases, such as a hyena who had a jaw which was broken. Ishaan explains that the process of treating a wild animal, especially as ferocious as a hyena or a leopard is quite simple because firstly it is done by professionals. Secondly, it is done by tranquilizing the animal which puts it into a deep sleep for a few hours and within those hours, the animal is treated and then after a few days of rehabilitation, it is sent back to the wild.

Another interesting case that Ishaan explains is where the forest department rescued orphan leopard cubs. According to Ishaan, there are a few checks that happen before declaring any baby animal as an orphan, and then they are taken for rehabilitation with an environment created for them to resemble the warmth that their mother would have given them. But he explains that these animals become quite weak if they are left in the wild and they might die because most, rather all of the fighting skills, skills that are required to sustain in the wild, are taught by the mother in the first two years of their babies’s life, but without the mother, the young animals become clueless, and in such cases, they are usually sent to sanctuaries or zoo, which keep them safe. Along with such issue has worked with flamingos, sea turtles, monkeys, and many more.

Ishaan has not only worked here in Maharashtra, but has also worked internationally as he serves as a board member for the International Veterinary Students Association, which is an organisation of all the veterinary students around the world. This organisation consists of around 80 countries with more than 25,000 students that take part in this. Ishaan expresses his gratitude and happiness for all the opportunities that he has gotten because of this association. He’s had the opportunity to go to a lot of countries for international events, for exchange programs, which has helped his journey even more. He was also part of the World Organisation for Animal Health Conference which happened in Geneva. For someone so young to be a part of such a global conference which contains multitudes at different levels of importance is a big deal and this says a lot about Ishaan, about his passion for this profession, as well as his passion and love for the animals.

When it comes to other countries, Ishaan mentions that it is very interesting to see what the countries are doing for conservation. For example when he talks about the Amazon rainforest, which is the mother of all forests in the world, but he says that the people that live right on the border around the Amazon, are not quite bothered about conservation work for them. They have grown in relation to the Amazon but comparing it to the Indians who live around the forest, they are quite harmonious in the forest, coexisting with nature.

As a lover of animals and wildlife, to be in the Amazon for five days, were the best days for Ishaan in his entire life.

“If you are a kid who likes animals who has grown up with everything that I’ve grown up with, being in the Amazon, the mother of all Jungle is basically where you want to be, right?”

Ishaan narrates his story of how he ended up in the Amazon. He was in Peru for a conference of the International Veterinary Student Association at Lima and from there with other students, he went into the Amazon where he had to take a boat, trek and travel a lot to reach a place set up by the rescuers there. It was a very thrilling experience for Ishaan as he had no electricity, no range, spotting pythons or big tarantula spiders in the vicinity.

When I asked about his favourite species that he has ever seen, Ishaan brings it back to his number one animal, the Tiger. For him, the Tiger is everything and especially spotting a tiger in the Indian forest and sanctuaries, for Ishaan which are the best and his most favourite, is very exciting.

Very recently, Ishaan has started his own organisation called Youth4Wild. His main idea for starting this organisation was to bridge the gap between different experts coming from different fields because when it comes to conservation, it’s not just that biologists are involved. Environmentalists, people from media, people from law and many more sectors are involved in this entire process. But Ishaan realised that there was no organisation or medium that was communicating and connecting these experts and for that, and to give the youth who are working in these fields, more opportunities and gain hands-on experience, Ishaan started this organisation. He revealed that they have started a podcast with leading conservationists in India, which is a very exciting project that is going to expand in future.

When it comes to wildlife, it is very difficult to ignore human intervention, which is unfortunately increasing. Ishaan strongly addresses that it is very important to find a balance or the sweet spot to make sure that development also happens because the people that are living in the jungle, the tribes, the villagers that are surrounding these jungle are quite poor and at the same time under the name of development, forest and animal should not be troubled. There should be more policies that should come up to create that balance.

“There should be a coexistence between nature, animals, and humans.”

The entire Earth is not just for humans. It is for these animals whether wild or domestic and Ishaan also agrees that we should start, at a very local personal level, initiatives like putting water bowls on the street for animals, food for animals and water and food for birds. It is very important that we start such initiatives to help these lonely animals, especially in dying times of scorching heat. Coming to human intervention, Ishaan also talks about the dilemma of conducting a rescue. If the animal is injured due to natural causes, it is very rare that there is rescue work that happens, but if it is because of human intervention, then it is very important to do this rescue work.

Ishaan talks about practicality and being practical rather than stating the policies on paper. Currently, India is facing a big issue with stray dogs and as an animal doctor and rescuer, Ishaan suggests that we should start sterilisation camps and vaccination camps for these dogs, which will help to reduce the population in the future. In the city of Mumbai, this problem is quite less as many dogs in many localities are sterilised, but in villages lies the problem with a lot of feral dogs.

I had a cat of mine die because of jaundice. We nursed her and she was being treated in the Mumbai Veterinary Hospital, but after a month, unfortunately she passed away. But I realized during this time that since my family lives close to the hospital, we were able to reach there within 15 to 20 minutes, but I remember that there were people coming from far away places. So this is a question of lack of government hospitals and clinics. Because you either have this Hospital in Parel or extremely expensive private institutions who take a lot of fees for basic surgeries like neutering. Ishaan suggests that the government should come up with more areas to help animals, pets, and strays alike.

Dr. Ishaan Acharya is a great example, of following your heart and passion, and actually enjoying the work that you want to do. His trajectory of getting into this profession, attending conferences internationally, having hands-on experience in fields that interest him shows that when you put your mind and do something that you actually and truly love, you can do it with any obstacle, coming in your way.
