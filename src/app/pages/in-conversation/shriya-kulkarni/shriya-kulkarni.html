The journey from playing to teaching

An interview with Shriya Kulkarni on teaching gymnastics

When the whole nation has multiple sports that get hidden under the sunlight and one of them is gymnastics, especially rhythmic gymnastics. Gymnastics comes in all sorts of types, whether it is aerial gymnastics or rhythmic gymnastics. It is a very useful sport, wherein there is training of the body to make it as flexible as possible to achieve discipline with your body and mental and physical strength. Out of all the types of gymnastics, rhythmic gymnastics is one that is really pushing and turning the game.

It is a Russian sport which consists of only women gymnasts wherein the sport combines ballet, dance and the art of gymnastics to perform routines on a floor using different apparatus like the hoop, ball, clubs, ribbon or rope which is set to music. Rhythmic gymnastics is a sport which emphasises flexibility, agility, and precision. Usually the routines are scored based on difficulty, artistry and execution.

Out of the key important players who played for India at different championships is a good friend of mine, Shriya Kulkarni. She started her journey in gymnastics at a very young age. She was only four years old when her parents decided to put her in different hobby classes. They tried singing, dancing, and drawing, but according to Shriya, she was never keen to do singing, drawing or dancing, but nonetheless, she went for dance class to Phulawa Khamkar, who is a very celebrated choreographer in the film industry. Phulawa Khamkar told Shriya’s mother that her calling is not in dance, but in gymnastics because she had flexibility and the physique to perform artistic gymnastics. It was since then that her journey started in gymnastics.

She has been playing for many years and has represented India at the Asian championship where she played as a junior in the under 16 category at the age of 14. Shriya was never found in school. She was always in class or playing championships and winning medals. For her, her proudest moment was when she got the blazer of India, when she had to go represent the nation at the Asian championships. Shriya had been learning under Varsha Upadhye, a rhythmic gymnastics coach who has created a name for her at the global forum.

It was until 2019 that Shriya had to stop her play for a temporary time due to a knee injury that happened. Post the knee injury it became difficult for Shriya to get back into the sports, but it was as if destiny had different plans for her. She has now started teaching smaller kids gymnastics at the Sane Guruji school in Dadar. It was only then that she could understand the point of view as a teacher to teach her children.

According to her, gymnastics is a sport that should be inculcated from a very young age because it helps the muscles to fully develop because there is no scope of flexibility to increase after a certain age. The peak of a gymnastic performance actually happens from 14 years of age to a maximum 22 years, she says that as a rhythmic gymnast’s point of view, girls go through a lot of changes in their body and in the 6 to 7 years, it becomes very difficult to maintain the physique post these years.

Shriya was naturally gifted with long limbs which allowed her and gave her flight to do rhythmic gymnastics, but there are kids that she’s teaching who are shorter in height. Shriya criticises and states that the beauty standards are very much applicable for gymnasts as well in the way of physical standards.

She digresses that there are many controversies regarding these physical standards, such as the players should be tall, should be thin, but not too thin, but they should also have long arms and a long neck.

“So you can just break the bar and you can climb over it, and we can break the stereotype”

When it comes to teaching small kids, this is a very crucial time in their life as education plays a major role for them. Shriya has first hand knowledge of how it can be stressful to juggle and balance education and sports at the same time. She says that sports is very unpredictable and for small kids right now it is very important for them to give much attention to education first. Due to the unpredictable behaviour of sports, it can very much happen that one fine day, you can just stop playing because of some reason. She says that as an athlete, one might never know when the sporting life is going to end because of some injury or any other issue. A regular day in her life of teaching starts with her kids attending school completely and in the evening around 6 or 7 o’clock is when the school kids come to warm up and start with their exercises, which is very important for flexibility and strength and conditioning, and then the actual routine and choreography starts.

Shriya had a very hard time balancing sports and education as rightly said by her because sports is quite unpredictable. Fortunately, for her, her school was very helpful in providing a lot of support in her sporting life.

“They were like Gods for me because they helped me so much”.

She acknowledged her friends and teachers alike who helped her understand the concepts and provide her with study material.

She compares sports and studies in a very unique way. In a routine, one has to perform in rhythmic gymnastics, which is only 1 minute and 30 seconds, but the training goes on for months and years only for it to dial down in those 1 minute and 30 seconds. She compares it similarly to studying. One does study for all the months and semesters to give that one exam of a few hours. Shriya had a competition right before the 10th board exams and she had gone to Uzbekistan, but very smartly she chose to take her books and study there. In this way, she emphasises on more of a balance and being smart in ways to handle your studying and other life choices with sports.

Shriya’s students have been participating in multiple competitions and have numerous medals and awards, bringing her a sense of pride.

As a teacher, she talks about the difficulties one has to go through, especially during competitions colliding with festivities at home. Her kids had recently gone for a competition in Kerala in October 2025. She recalls the training as rigorous as she made her students train every morning as well as every evening.

“I think it’s very important for small kids to understand where they are standing and how much responsibility they have on their shoulders”

When it comes to teaching especially teaching young children, a major part of the relation between a student and a teacher always consists of the parent. Since the children are young, they are not smart and mature enough to make certain decisions for themselves. Hence the role of the parent in such times comes as large. Shriya emphasises on patience and explaining it to the parents of the children that participating in competitions doesn’t happen overnight, sports doesn’t happen overnight and winning medals doesn’t happen overnight. She recalls moments of reassurance that she has had to give to parents in order that they don’t take the name of the child out of the class.

Shriya says that it is just like school, you have to go in a systematic order from 1st grade to 2nd grade to 3rd grade. Similarly, you have to first start with the local competitions then go to state level then regional then national and then international, but you have to maintain that consistency, discipline, as well as dedication to the sports.

The biggest twist in Shriya’s teaching career is that her being a rhythmic gymnast, she is teaching aerobic gymnastics, which is a different field for her. She puts focus on the importance of trusting the coach because the coach does put in a lot of efforts and hard work to make sure that the kids are going to succeed in the long run. For Shriya, it is very important for the parents to know that how much hard work goes behind the scenes and how much effort it takes for the coach to bring the child to that optimum level.

When talking about balancing education and sports in this juncture, it is not about school and sports, but for Shriya, it continues with her masters in sports management and taking classes at the same time. Her college happens in the afternoon, and she has to go directly from college to her class to teach. It sometimes gets difficult for her to reach on time, but she pushes through since she has a responsibility that every coach has on their shoulder to make sure that the kids practice daily and their practice goes smoothly without any difficulties.

When asked about sports management and its benefit to actually managing sports like the one that she teaches, Shriya connects it to the role and experience that she has gotten from teaching and how it might help her when she gets a permanent job. Shriya explains that sports management in India is quite still niche, and there are no other jobs as large as there are in other fields like cricket. She brings out a very important point of building a strong community and connections, especially in a sports field, which is quite niche in India.

Shriya today attributes her strength and her role as a teacher, and as a gymnast to her coaches who motivated her and helped her understand different aspects of sports as well as life and taught different elements like time management, dedication, and discipline. Shriya at the very same time says that it is very important for the coach to have a soft spot for students and build a good rapport with them because she knows what it is to be in the student’s position at that particular age. She talks about how important it is to have a very good connection with your students, especially in times of injuries, in times of help and how it can help in the long run. Shriya then brings focus about her injury that happened in 2019 and how it completely changed her life. It was her coach who expected and wanted her to go back to playing, but her doctor had told her not to take any physical strain.

For Shriya, it is very important to get in that sense of management and dedication from her students so that it will help them positively. She has learnt a lot as a student many years ago and now is using that knowledge to train her children into fantastic performers. Although she might be young, her story talks about resilience and usage of knowledge at the correct time and from the correct experiences one can go and push someone to do better.
