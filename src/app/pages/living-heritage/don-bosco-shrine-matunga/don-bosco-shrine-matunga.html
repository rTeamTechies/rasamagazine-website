Finding serenity and calmness in the heart of Matunga.

The Don Bosco Shrine of Madonna in Matunga is more than just a place of worship, it's a cultural beauty!

Out of all the localities in Mumbai Matunga is one that is quite calm and peaceful with a lot of cultural and old neighbourhoods. And one of the most important and iconic Catholic shrines of Mumbai lies here in Matunga itself. It is known as the Shrine of Mary Help of Christians or the Shrine of Don Bosco’s Madonna. It is an important church across all the devotees in Mumbai and India. It is considered as an important centre for Marian devotion, Salesians, spirituality, art, architecture, and Catholic Heritage.

The shrine is not just a shrine. It is a sacred place of prayer and reflection, but at the same time, it brings a sense of calmness to anyone who visits. Its architecture is quite inspiring with a fitting golden statue of Mary at the top with beautiful stained glass artwork, and an underground crypt and a rich spiritual history. All of these aspects have made it an important Catholic landmark in the city and thousands of devout Catholics and non-Catholics alike, continue to visit the shrine every day, seeking calmness, peace, prayer, and spiritual comfort.

Back to the origin of the shrine, it can be linked to the growth of the Salesians of Don Bosco in Mumbai. In 1937, the Salesian congregation acquired land in Matunga from the Bombay Municipal Corporation to put forward and established an educational and religious institution in the rapidly developing suburb. At the time, the vision for the shrine itself emerged under the leadership of Reverend Father Aurelius Maschio, who was an Italian priest. He played an extremely important and transformative role in the development of the Don Bosco campus in Matunga. He had a dream of building a grand Marian shrine dedicated to Mary Help of Christians, who is the Patroness, especially venerated by the Salesians.

On 7th December 1954, the foundation stone of the shrine was blessed and laid upon by Valerian Cardinal Gracias who was the most influential Catholic leader in India. The Bombay Municipal Corporation granted permission for the Shrine and officially in 1955, within just two years, the structure was completed all due to the efforts of Salesian priests, workers, architects, benefactors, and the local Catholics present at the time. Auxiliary Bishop Longinus Parreira blessed the shrine officially for public veneration on 5th August 1957.

In 1964, on 28th November Archbishop Hubert D’Rosario formally consecrated the church in the presence of seven Bishops during the 39th International Eucharistic Congress held in Mumbai, making it a major milestone in the history of the Shrine. A few days later from that event on 4th December 1964, Pope Paul VI gave a visit to the Shrine during his historic visit to India and blessed the statue of Mary Help of Christians used during the annual feast procession. This visit by the Pope was a very important one as it elevated the shrine's importance not only in Mumbai but also within the wider Indian Catholic community.

When we talk about this shrine, it is very important to acknowledge St. John Bosco. He was born in Italy in 1815 and was a Catholic priest, educator and a social reformer who dedicated entire life to welfare of poor and abandoned youth. He believed in the values of strong education, kindness, discipline, and spiritual guidance as different tools for transforming society into a better one.

In 1859, he founded the Salesians of Don Bosco officially known as the society of Saint Francis de Sales. The Salesians Congregation was one which focused on education, youth development, technical training and missionary work. Over the course of time the Salesians have spread across the world, including India with their established schools, colleges, churches and social institutions carrying forward the legacy of St. John Bosco.

This particular shine in Matunga reflects the philosophy of combining the aspect of spirituality with service to society. The Salesians not only built the shrine but also developed the surrounding educational institutions such as the school present there, that is the Don Bosco High school Matunga, which was first associated in Tardeo was shifted to the Matunga premises.

Pataki and Dadarkar were the prime architects, who were quite renowned at the time, behind the design of the shrine. They were also associated with other Don Bosco Institutions in Matunga as well. The classical Roman Church architecture and the Basilica of Mary Help of Christians in Turin, Italy was taken as an inspiration for the architecture style of the shrine in Matunga. The shrine in Turin is one that is closely associated with Don Bosco.

Reverend Father Aurelius Maschio was the principal visionary behind the entire project. His complete devotion to the Patroness and the Salesians mission helped shape the journey of the shrine. He continued serving the shrine and became one of the most influential figures in Mumbai. After his death in 1996, he was buried within the shrine premises, as a testament for his lifelong connection and dedication to the church.

The majestic Roman inspired structure is one that immediately stands out in the vicinity of Matunga. There is a traditional Latin cross layout which is followed, which is usually seen in classical European Cathedrals while the shrine exterior is made using golden brown Malad Granite, which gives it an ethereal and timeless appearance.

There is a 12 foot high gold plated statue of Mary Help of Christians placed atop the central dome, which is the most recognised feature of this particular Shrine. The statue was cast in Italy and was installed in 1957 and it is quite visible from several parts of Matunga and Wadala. A very fun fact about this statue is that it was put up with the help of cranes which were borrowed at the time from Film City.

At the entrance of the church, there is a large mosaic, which depicts Don Bosco with the Madonna, creating and symbolising the spiritual bond between the Salesians and Mary Help of Christians.

The interior of the shine is quite beautiful, and it truly is a testament to a place that gives that sense of peace and calmness. The main prayer hall is quite vast and it has the seating of around 800 worshippers with additional standing space for several hundred more. Italian imported marble of different shades and patterns are used for the entire interior. There is a sense of grandeur and serenity created because of the polished marble surfaces as well as the symmetry of the marble. Combining it with a high dome and chandelier gives the shine a very contemplated, calm, and peaceful ambience.

The prayer hall is one which today still preserves the traditional Catholic architectural elements such as the holy waterfront, communion reels, confession boxes and elevated sanctuary spaces. The shrine is one of the very few shrines that still maintain the pull pit from where the priest used to do the preaching, which is an elevated dais.

Another beautiful element of the shrine is the stained glass windows which show the Mystery of the Rosary. These panels of the glass windows are regarded as some of the finest examples of ecclesiastical stained glass art in the city of Mumbai. Italian artist Peter Flavio was the one who created this glasswork and along with the mystery of the rosary, it also contains certain scenes from the life of Don Bosco. Three prominent Marian stain glass panels stand tall above the main altar.

The story is that these glass pieces were tempered in kilns in Italy in a very special way, and later on, they were shipped to India in segments before they were reassembled as one whole inside the shine. Truly, it is a magnificent sight, especially when the sun shines through the windows, creating a dramatic reflection of all the colours of red, blue, saffron and green across the interiors covered in marble. From the 1960s, the windows have been accredited with a symphony of colours within the church. They not only function as windows, but also tell stories about the shrine.

The spiritual centre of the shrine is the sanctuary and the main altar which is elevated prominently beneath the central dome. The altar is designed in such a way that it draws the attention of the worshippers towards the Eucharistic celebration. At the backdrop of the main altar, there is a Marian imagery with stained glass artistry which is dedicated to Mary Help of Christians. The entire altar is decorated by floral decorations, candles and liturgical ornaments, which are regularly arranged, especially during days of feast and novenas. The altar serves as a main focal point during the Saturday services attended by multiple devotees across the city.

One of the most interesting facts about this shrine is the crypt that lies below it. It is quite one of the lesser known sections and it possesses a completely different atmosphere from the grandeur of the main prayer hall above. It is quite deeply silent and dimly lit, creating a space for private prayer, reflection and intimate ceremonies. The walls of the crypt are so special because they contain relics of numerous saints which are enclosed in these decorative cases. There are more mosaic panels which depict the life of Christ and Don Bosco. A very interesting factor is that these panels are very special because they are created in a way that they are anatomically correct. The crypt is such that it can accommodate almost 200 people and is usually used for smaller masses, Thanksgiving services and weddings.

Don Bosco’s Shrine of Madonna is one shrine out of many, which is Mumbai’s most important pilgrimage center for Marian devotion. Numerous and thousands of devotees visit the shrine, seeking blessings, healing and much more. The shrine offers much more than just prayer because anyone from any walk of life can come to reflect, find inner peace and just lose themselves in the calmness that the shrine gives. The shrine is not merely a church, but it is a historical and cultural landmark, which is quite significant in the history of Mumbai‘s culture and architecture. Its location in Matunga gives it more of an importance because it is known for its religious, cultural and old neighbourhoods, and the Shrine of Don Bosco’s Madonna adds to its beauty.

A dear friend of mine and a Salesian, Ethan Fortes, says “Don Bosco in Matunga is the mother house, and the shrine is a place, which is a centrepoint for us and from this place we move to other places, so we all might be working different areas, but we come back to this mother house”. Even after six decades of the inauguration, the shrine is one that remains spiritually alive. Still, its bell still rings over Matunga and it’s still in the afternoon sunlight while the Golden Madonna watches over the skyline, acting as a symbol of faith, hope and tradition in Mumbai.
