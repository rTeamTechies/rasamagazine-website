Shivaji Park - a place of community and warmth.

The heart of Dadar and the number one love of Dadarkars!

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Park also known as Shivaji Park is one of the city’s most iconic public spaces which are located in Dadar. Dadar itself is an essential and historically rich neighbourhood of the city. Shivaji Park is spread across approximately 1,12,000 meter square area and is the largest open public park in Mumbai’s island city and serves as an important space in urban life, which brings together history, culture, sports, and politics. The significance of this park runs beyond its physical boundaries due to its role as a social and political stage as well as a nursery for cricket excellence and a community hub for generations of Mumbaikars.

The park was developed in 1925 during the British colonial period as a part of the urban planning initiatives which were happening in Dadar. This idea for such an initiative was conceived through a planned suburb to decongest the Southern parts of Bombay at the time. The park was initially called Mahim Park, but was later changed after Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. From the time it was created the park was always seen as a large public space for recreation and community interaction.

Over the course of time it has evolved into a very important landmark of the city which embodies both the colonial urban planning and post independence cultural identity with the help of a statue of Shivaji Maharaj in the park, this idea is reinforced. The Shivaji Maharaj statue was installed in 1966 as a symbol of leadership.

The most prominent identity of this park is that of its association with cricket. Shivaji Park has nurtured several legendary cricketers who have gone to represent the country at the highest level such as Sachin Tendulkar, Sunil Gavaskar, Ajit Wadekar, Vinod Kambli and Ravi Shastri. With the help of nets all around the park, there is an ecosystem which is created, especially for sports along with institutions like Shivaji Park Gymkhana, Samarth Mandir and multiple places, making this Park a training nursery for aspiring athletes.

Today, thousands of students from all over Mumbai come here to train just like a lot of these legendary cricketers did, creating a huge cricket legacy in the park itself. The park continues to be an open space to facilitate and help young players practice daily.

Shivaji Park is a place not only for cricket but for other sports like football and tennis especially at the Gymkhana or even badminton and many more. This allows for kids and professional athletes from all over the city to travel to play at this park.

Beyond the role of sports, Shivaji Park plays an important role in the everyday lives of Dadar residents. The place serves as a large communal space for people to gather for morning walks, yoga, festivals, and social interactions which has been going on for multiple decades. The location of the park is such that it is surrounded by residential neighbourhoods, schools, temples and cultural institutions making it an integral part of local life.

The circumference of the Park is filled with landmarks, such as the Udyan Ganesh Temple, Kali Mata Mandir, Scout Pavilion, and gardens for small children as well as for aged people, which contribute to a wide social fabric. With the help of the trees that run across the pathways, it provides a rare sense of calmness in a very densely populated city, making it a cherished and sought after space for relaxing and recreation.

The park is decorated seasonally as per seasons or religious events or festivals. This surrounding area of the park itself adds more to the heritage of the park with areas like Chaitya Bhoomi, the Glow Garden or as it is lovingly called as Narali Baug, etc

Shivaji Park and politics have always had a connection for a long period of time. Due to its open space, the park has been used to host numerous political meetings, rallies and public gatherings, making it a symbol of democratic expression in Mumbai. After Independence Shivaji Park became closely associated with a lot of regional Maharashtra politics, particularly the party of Shiv Sena. The party’s founder, Balasaheb Thackeray, used to hold some of his earliest and most influential rallies in this park, making it quite symbolic. Shiv Sena holds its annual Dussehra Meet traditionally at the park, which has become a major political event, drawing large crowds, and also shaping the political discourse in Maharashtra.

However, this prominence of politics has also led to a lot of debates and controversy among the local residents who have been raising concerns of noise, crowd management, and traffic happening due to large gatherings and also how all of these are affecting the environment of the park. Shivaji Park is deeply embedded in the identity of Dadar by describing it as the heart of Dadar. The park works as a democratic space, making it accessible to people from all walks of life and all backgrounds.

Right from children playing cricket to elderly residents taking their evening walk, in a large metropolitan city, it is very rare to find such large open grounds, making this place significant and scarce in the urban ecosystem. Shivaji Park and the social life around it is shaped by the proximity of the park to a lot of iconic landmarks, such as the Dadar Railway Station, the Dadar Chowpatty as well as Siddhivinayak Temple. This connectivity adds a lot of meaning to the existence of Shivaji Park

Despite the important role of this Park, it has faced several challenges such as overuse, pollution and infrastructural wear. There have been recent discussions which are focused on improving the facilities, maintaining cleanliness and preserving the park’s original character. While adapting contemporary needs there has been a lot of restoration process that has started in the park with a lot of painting, etc. There have been efforts taken by the Brihan Mumbai Municipal Corporation, and local communities and NGOs to address these challenges through renovation projects and better maintenance both for people who are coming there for recreation as well as sports.

Today, Shivaji Park signifies as more than just an open ground in the city of Mumbai. It is a symbol of the city’s history, culture and collective identity right from its origin as a planned colonial park to its evolution into a place for sports, politics and common life to come together. In a city where space is considered as a luxury, Shivaji Park remains as a rare and an invaluable asset, a place where history and modernism, and where personal memories emerge with public events and where the spirit of Mumbai and especially Dadar residents emerges.
