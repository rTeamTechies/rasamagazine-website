Why Mumbai deserves better in terms of arts?

An audience member’s experience and opinion on Mumbai based Guru and dance schools.

Before I start this article, I want to preface it with a disclaimer that should go for every article that is written by me, Anjaneya Mulekar, that I have no experience as a student learning different, classical dances or classical music, I don’t know any technical knowledge about classical dance or classical music, but I am an avid audience member and an admirer of classical dance and classical music who has attended multiple events of the same genres. After a few events you set some expectations for the coming performances by different artists. I will not acknowledge the name, but I have certain standards to compare the performances to. And I know it’s wrong, but I think we do that in every aspect of life, don’t we? Unknowingly, we compare things to a certain standard and I know it’s not possible for every performance to meet that standard, but at least be close to it. Classical dance is such that under eight classical dances, you have different styles of performing and every guru has their own way of teaching their students, but at the end of the day, the end product that is the performance, that the audience are going to witness on stage has to be well organised, well coordinated and meaningful enough.

Classical dance has a sort of hub of large existence in the southern parts of India, especially cities like Chennai or Bengaluru. These places serve as a space for classical dance and dancers to exist at a very strong bond. Cities like these are spaces where you have sabhas or institutional spaces at every corner and there are certain performances always going on, and you can simply walk in, sit and enjoy the performance and leave. That is why many dancers often then shift to the southern parts of India to continue their career as a professional dancer. But when you compare something like this to a city like Mumbai, which is a metropolitan city that is more associated with finance and business, rather than arts, spaces like Mumbai, unfortunately do not give that much priority to the arts, but that being said looking at the population of Mumbai, it can be safely, said that there are around millions of people who to show up for arts, classical events, and support them.

When I went to my first performance at the NCPA or at the NMACC or even the Chembur Fine Arts Society, I was astonished to see the number of people show up for such beautiful classical performances, especially in a city that is busy and it is so hard to get time from away from your job.

Despite Mumbai’s personality of being a finance associated city, there are many gurus who are living here and have their schools or classes going on in this particular city of Mumbai which to me was also quite astonishing.

All the dancers that I had been seeing until this year were dancers or gurus who were not from Mumbai and had come to Mumbai to showcase their talent and give a chance to Mumbai audiences to witness their talent. I have seen a few performances of Mumbai based gurus and their students, and I think it’s safe to say that, few of these gurus and their schools and their students, lack something.

I might not be in a position to comment on talent or artistic skills, but as an audience member who has seen countless performances by now and not just in real life performances, virtual videos as well, one can immediately see if the dancer is struggling or not, whether they are taught well or not. I think somewhere that there are certain aspects that can only be felt and experienced, and can be learnt when you’re in a city that is so culturally induced like Chennai or Bengaluru. No one knows what exactly goes on in these classes of these few gurus in Mumbai, but when the students perform it is safe to understand and acknowledge that it is not correct.

I visited a recent performance, and I was not happy with what I witnessed from the dance gurus and students based in Mumbai. It was a very lacklustre, and there was no sense of coordination, no sense of discipline, no sense of the true sense of the dance and no sense of stage experience. And I am pretty sure anyone would have been able to notice such mistakes, even if they were watching them for the first time. This made me realise that Mumbai gurus have a long way to go compared to other gurus based in other cities.

And I don’t think the audiences are at fault. Because as I said there are thousands of people who will show up to performances every day, even if it’s either a weekday or a weekend and at any point of the day whether it’s 2 PM in the afternoon or at 8 PM in the night, people will show up for classical performances. Mumbai is not lacking in audience, but it is lacking in the talent that is coming from these dance schools in Mumbai. This is not a sentiment of hatred, rather a sentiment of deep thought when it comes to arts because then it becomes wrong for the gurus to complain, criticise the audiences for not showing up, which is completely wrong, especially in Mumbai.

Take an example of Bharatanatyam, which has different styles called as Banis, one can say that every guru has adopted a different style, whether it is the Bharatnrityam style, whether it is the Tanjore Quartet, whether it is the Melattur style and many more, but the basic movements are the same, the basic vocabulary of the dance is the same. There are a few changes here and there between the styles, but what is being performed is the same, so you cannot really blame the style of the dance on the dancer's bad performance. The dancer over here is at fault and moreover the guru is at fault because it is the Guru’s responsibility to say that and point out the wrongs and correct them.

Again, no one knows what happens in these classes at the same time. Don’t know if dancers have these conversations within themselves. I have always wondered about the thoughts and opinions of gurus and dancers who hail from different cities beyond Mumbai when they witness the performances of gurus and students coming from schools based in Mumbai. I know the Natya Kala Conference which happens at Krishna Gana Sabha every year is a very important conference, but I don’t know if this is something that is being discussed.

Again, this is not hatred in any sense, but rather some encouragement. Again, this is not a generalisation. It is about some gurus that at least I have witnessed. And this feeling that I had has been bubbling up for a very long time and I decided to write on this topic for Rasa Thinks, because after all this is something to think about, is it not?
