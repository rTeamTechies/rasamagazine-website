“This... ‘stuff’? Oh, okay. I see, you think this has nothing to do with you. You go to your closet and you select, I don’t know, that lumpy blue sweater, for instance, because you’re trying to tell the world that you take yourself too seriously to care about what you put on your back. But what you don’t know is that that sweater is not just blue, it’s not turquoise, it’s not lapis, it’s actually cerulean. You’re also blithely unaware of the fact that in 2002, Oscar de la Renta did a collection of cerulean gowns. And then I think it was Yves Saint Laurent... wasn’t it who showed cerulean military jackets? I think we need a jacket here. And then cerulean quickly showed up in the collections of eight different designers. And then it filtered down through the department stores and then trickled on down into some tragic ‘casual corner’ where you, no doubt, fished it out of some clearance bin. However, that blue represents millions of dollars and countless jobs, and it’s sort of comical how you think that you’ve made a choice that exempts you from the fashion industry when, in fact, you’re wearing a sweater that was selected for you by the people in this room... from a pile of ‘stuff.’”

What do you call a film that not only provided entertainment but has completely changed an entire culture for an entire generation on the way it views fashion, films, the working industry? What do you call a film that has completely birthed an entire generation of iconic scenes? This was the feeling that came 20 years ago when the first film from The Devil Wears Prada was released in 2006.

Photo credits - Amazon.in

Originally The Devil Wears Prada is a book series written by Lauren Weisberger, who was a personal assistant of Anna Wintour at Vogue at that time. She wrote about three books, creating fictional characters, more similar to the ones that are there in real life, and who knew that these books would become an iconic piece of art?

In 2006, the first film based on the first book of The Devil Wears Prada was released. It was directed by David Frankel with a screenplay written by Aline Brosh McKenna. It was a film that at that time was something that was going to be changing, creating rumours amongst the fashion industry. From various sources, it is said that everyone was too scared to make this film because it was based on the life of the most powerful and influential person in the fashion industry in the entire world, Anna Wintour. But once the film came out, everything changed. This film gave a way to look at life in a more fashionable way because don’t we need a little bit of fashion in our lives?

Photo credits - Film Streams

The first film revolves around the character of Miranda Priestley played by the iconic Meryl Streep. Miranda was shown as this cold emotionally unavailable boss to her two assistants, Emily Charlton played by Emily Blunt and Andrea played by Anne Hathaway. The first film was just a documentary. One was trying to see how this new girl Andrea was adapting to this new job, new people, and her very new boss. It was obviously with the help of her colleague, Nigel played by Stanley Tucci that she was able to gain some control and confidence at her job. The first film was such that it did not involve a very climatic incident which usually movies have. The first film showed the everyday life of Andrea Sachs, trying to live a life with a very powerful boss and in this very different company of runway. But nonetheless, the first film obviously was nothing short of life changing, in every way.

The film created an iconic legacy, especially with the song Vogue by Madonna used in the film. The film created hope and inspiration for a lot of people, Gen Z audiences, thinking that working and corporate life might be fun after all, especially with fashion involved.

But we never knew that we needed a second film and that’s when the rumour started spreading. It was two years ago that for a series on YouTube called Actors on Actors, created by Variety, that Emily Blunt and Anne Hathaway reappeared as a pair. It was at that time when people started gossiping and spreading that maybe there might be a second film coming out, but there was no confirmation or whatsoever. It was only until last year in the second half, that the OG cast were seen in New York. That is the exact time when people found out that there is a second film being made, although the second film is not based on the second book series. It is a completely different idea written by the writer Aline Brosh McKenna, it is still a film that is much much relevant today.

Photo credits - Prime Video

The Devil Wears Prada 2 is not just a film. It is more of a reflection of what is going on in, especially in the publication industry with everything becoming digital and with use of AI. The story starts with a scandal that leaves Miranda Priestly in the gutter, almost, but it is Anne Hathaway who comes to save the magazine back as Andrea, and tries her level best to bring out journalistic ideologies and believes as the new features editor, but they have no success because Runway is still down.

In the meantime, we find out that Emily is now working at Dior at a very high position. This is a very interesting time for Miranda in the film because she is about to get promoted to the Global Head of Content of Elias Clarke. However, the owner of Elias Clark, which is the parent company of Runway dies unexpectedly leaving everything to his son who is someone not bothered about the company nor fashion nor publications at all. Andrea realises that this is a difficult time and things might go south, and subsequently there are negative changes that happen to the publication, there are lots of budget cuts, lots of money is reduced and everything is online.

Soon enough, the entire cast goes to Italy in the film for a very important event and Andrea in order to save Runway decides to seek the help of Emily who is dating Benji a rich millionaire who agrees to buy Elias Clark but something that Andrea doesn’t know that Emily will become the new editor and Global Head of Content if Benji buys the company. Miranda gets an inkling of something happening behind her back, and that’s when her and Andrea join hands to seek out Sasha, Benji’s ex wife, played by Lucy Liu, who is also a millionaire and who Andrea reaches out to get an interview in the first half of the film to get some hype for Runway.

Finally in the end, Sasha agrees to buy Elias Clark, handing over the position of Global Head of Content to Miranda and everything turning out to be well.

The main turning point was Miranda’s character showing vulnerability and her love for runway when she breaks down after finding out Benji, played by Justin Theroux, will reduce the publication to almost nothing with the use of AI.

Comparing the two things it can obviously be seen that this film has a climax and turning point where the scandal leads to a lot of chaos and also Miranda’s character in this film is much more vulnerable, open. There is one particular scene where she calls Andrea to her townhouse for a small party. In those scenes, it can be seen that Miranda is not just a strong cold person. She obviously has a heart and is expressing it more in this particular film, especially with her new partner in the film played by Kenneth Branagh. Stanley Tucci’s character remains as iconic as ever with sassy lines and his taste of fashion, and obviously, it was so heartwarming to see that friendship between Nigel and Andrea rekindle again in this film.

Photo credits - W Magazine

One more very interesting aspect of this film is that it is obviously based in the very new generation and with a lot of woke behaviour. Simon Ashley’s character Amari, is the new assistant for Miranda and she is there to keep Miranda in line so that she doesn’t get cancelled for saying anything out of the blue, which was not the case in the first film, where Miranda was saying things that would have cancelled her today. But I think that is what gave the movie the charm that it has today.

To talk about the fashion of the film is something very interesting. The first part of the film was very grey toned with the film, colour grading being very bright and almost artificial. It almost looked like an advertisement compared to the first film. The colour grading in the first one was impeccable, but this film felt a little bit absurd in the range of colour grading. Secondly, the film had a lot of corporate clothes in the first few scenes, which was a bit disappointing. It was only after a few scenes that Miranda‘s fashion changed, Emily‘s character was wearing more fashionable clothes, and obviously once the entire cast was in Italy, the fashion was just beyond, but I wish the corporate scenes could have made use into fashionable clothes, comparing it to the first film that had just impeccable and archival clothes, which would have definitely made a difference. I also think that the use of grey should have been a little less because at one point, the buildings in the background, this road and the cars and the outfits were the same colour which made no sense at all. One of my most favourite moments, when it comes to the fashion part, is that the iconic cerulean sweater by Andrea makes an iconic appearance at the very end, making it like a bittersweet ending. I think the best outfit was the very first scene where we get to see Miranda again in the brilliant red gown with the red heels, which I’m guessing are Prada. Of course they should be Prada and why not?

For someone like me, this one was definitely refreshing because the topics that it dealt with such as online publication changes, use of AI. All of that felt very current, relatable and common. It was also very different and very interesting to see the characters deal with these issues that the world is facing today specially publication industry where everything is becoming online, but at the same time, the iconic value remains and I think this film has gained even much more momentum because it has earned more than $546.2 million worldwide which was more than the first film joint.

For me, this film remains as a cultural reset for today, and I am hoping that there will be a third film very soon because I feel this needs to be a series.
